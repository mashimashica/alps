# Handover Understanding Confirmation

## Purpose

Help the people in an operational handover expose and resolve consequential differences in what they understand about the work, while keeping unresolved matters and limits of evidence visible.

## Outcomes

- Each consequential handover item has a current, source-grounded account of the work, its known owner, timing or trigger, and relevant conditions, with missing values explicit.
- Commitments, proposals, read-backs, confirmations, corrections, completed work, and unresolved matters remain distinguishable.
- Consequential ambiguities, mismatches, missing authority, and missing evidence are expressed as answerable questions to an appropriate person.
- The current user can tell what is safe to rely on, what remains open, and how unresolved matters affect dependent work.
- Later replies or corrections can revise the affected understanding without silently preserving superseded conclusions.

## Activities & Tasks

The following Tasks are required within the supplied exchange and operational facts. They can be revisited whenever new evidence arrives.

### Establish the evidence boundary

1. Identify the speakers, the order of their statements, and any explicitly supplied operational facts or local excerpts.
2. Preserve uncertainty about recording coverage, speaker identity, missing context, or ambiguous references. Resolve phrases such as “that one,” “the usual time,” or “they” only when the available context supports one reading; otherwise keep the competing readings or ask what was meant.
3. Treat the latest explicit correction as the current statement for its stated scope, and mark the affected earlier statement as superseded. Do not assume that the other person noticed or accepted the correction.

### Form the current understanding

1. Identify the consequential items: work to perform, owner, deadline or trigger, location or destination, dependencies, remaining work, exceptions, approval conditions, and claimed completion.
2. For each item, keep the content and its evidence state separate. Use distinctions such as:
   - **stated**: a person asserted a fact or instruction;
   - **committed**: the proposed owner explicitly accepted the work;
   - **read back, awaiting confirmation**: a person repeated an understanding but no relevant reply confirms it;
   - **confirmed**: the relevant person explicitly acknowledged the stated understanding;
   - **open or disputed**: the exchange leaves a material question or mismatch;
   - **superseded**: a later correction replaced the earlier statement within its scope.
3. Attribute each commitment to the person who accepted it. A suggestion, request, tentative possibility, or statement that an absent person was asked is not acceptance by that person.
4. Separate ownership of the work from ownership of asking or following up on a question. Do not assign either merely because a person mentioned the issue.
5. Record completion only from supplied evidence that the work occurred. A plan, commitment, permission, read-back, or polished summary is not evidence of completion.
6. Apply supplied authority and approval conditions to the affected action. A question asked of an authorized person remains open until that person explicitly answers it; a different person's confidence or silence cannot supply the approval.

### Check the understanding in conversation

1. Present a concise current understanding in ordinary language suited to speaking or messaging. Include owners, timing, remaining work, conditions, and evidence state where those distinctions matter; a fixed handover form is not required.
2. State material gaps and mismatches plainly, together with their effect on dependent work. Do not bury them inside a fluent summary.
3. Formulate the smallest useful read-back and questions for the people who can confirm the fact, accept the work, or grant the required approval. Name the intended respondent when multiple people are involved.
4. Ask the current user to confirm, correct, or obtain the missing answer when interaction is possible. Do not claim to contact an absent person.
5. If no further reply is available, provide the best supported current understanding, the exact pending questions, and the actions that remain blocked or conditional. Do not manufacture agreement to make the handover look complete.

### Incorporate replies and corrections

1. Interpret each new reply only within what it explicitly confirms, rejects, corrects, accepts, or completes. A general acknowledgment does not automatically answer every separate question.
2. Update the affected items, mark replaced understandings as superseded, and reconsider dependent owners, times, questions, permissions, and safe-to-rely-on judgments.
3. Repeat a focused read-back when a correction or partial answer could leave a consequential mismatch. Stop when the available evidence supports shared understanding or when the remaining open items and their effects have been made explicit.

## Inputs

- A live conversation or speaker-attributed transcript, optionally with local plain-text excerpts and explicitly supplied operational facts.
- Any supplied limitations on speaker identity, recording accuracy, chronology, or coverage.

## Outputs

- A source-grounded current understanding expressed in a form appropriate to the conversation.
- Focused read-back language or questions for confirmation and correction.
- An explicit account of unresolved items, superseded statements, authority limits, and effects on dependent work.

## Controls

Use explicitly supplied operational facts, authority rules, and approval conditions as the basis for judging what may proceed. Preserve the speakers' ordered statements as evidence of what was said; do not turn that evidence into stronger agreement or completion claims than it supports.

## Constraints

- Do not contact absent colleagues, update task systems, perform the handed-over work, or claim that any such action occurred unless the user separately authorizes and the environment supports it.
- Do not infer consent, acceptance, approval, or confirmation from silence, lack of contradiction, a question, or a read-back alone.
- Do not authorize an exception on behalf of someone else or weaken a supplied prohibition.
- Treat later evidence as a reason to revise affected conclusions rather than as an addition that leaves contradictions hidden.

## Exit Criteria

The current understanding and its evidence states are explicit; consequential mismatches have either been confirmed or corrected by appropriate people, or remain visible with focused pending questions and effects on dependent work.
