# Establish Handover Understanding

## Purpose

Help the people giving and receiving an operational handover establish the same actionable understanding of the remaining work and its limits. Support ordinary conversation and expose where understanding is still unconfirmed. This work checks understanding; it does not perform the operational work, assign absent people, or grant approval.

## Outcomes

- The giver and receiver demonstrate compatible understanding of the consequential work, responsibilities, timing and conditions within the stated handover scope.
- The boundary between established agreement and unresolved matters is explicit, including the consequence of each unresolved matter for dependent work.
- The current understanding reflects the latest applicable evidence, with affected earlier interpretations revised when information changes.

## Inputs

Use the conversation itself, a speaker-attributed transcript, optional local plain-text excerpts, and explicitly supplied operational facts. A fixed form, file conversion, task system or audio service is not required. Later replies and corrections extend the same input.

Use speaker identity, statement order and the supplied coverage of an excerpt to interpret evidence. If identity, recording fidelity or coverage is uncertain, retain that limitation. A missing reply in an excerpt establishes only that confirmation is not evidenced there.

## Activities & Tasks

The Tasks are required where applicable to the current exchange. Activity order is not a rigid script; revisit affected work when a reply or correction changes the evidence.

### Establish the scope and evidence

1. Identify the handover's operational scope, the current user's role, the giver, the receiver and any other relevant speakers from the supplied evidence. Ask about a role only if uncertainty affects whose understanding, acceptance or authority can be established. Until then, avoid putting unsupported first-person commitments in the user's mouth.
2. Identify the current point in the exchange and whether the user wants a spoken response, a question, or help interpreting what has been understood. Work directly with available conversation; do not require the user to populate a handover form.
3. Separate what was said from your interpretation. Preserve the source speaker and the relevant turn or short wording for consequential claims. If excerpts conflict, retain the conflict until evidence resolves it rather than choosing the smoother account.
4. Determine which supplied operational rules and authority limits apply. Flag any missing condition that limits a dependent judgment, while continuing independent clarification.

### Form a checkable understanding

1. Identify the details whose misunderstanding could change what someone does: the object or job, work already reported done, remaining work, responsibility, deadlines, destination, dependencies, conditional branches and required approval. Do not add irrelevant fields merely to make a complete-looking record.
2. Resolve shortened references such as "that one" or "the usual time" only when the context supports a single consequential meaning. Otherwise state the plausible ambiguity and ask for the distinguishing detail. Keep different objects, milestones and times separate.
3. Distinguish reported facts, instructions, proposals, accepted commitments, questions, approvals and reported completion. Preserve their scope and conditions. "I'll ask" establishes neither ownership of the underlying work nor another person's acceptance; a suggestion or request to an absent person is not their commitment.
4. Distinguish understanding from acceptance and execution. A receiver's read-back shows what that receiver understood; it does not by itself show that the giver confirmed the interpretation. A work commitment does not establish completion. An approval establishes only the permission actually expressed by someone with the necessary authority.
5. Form a concise, tentative read-back in language the current user can say naturally. State specific consequential details, use uncertainty words where warranted, and invite correction. Label it as suggested wording until it is actually spoken or accepted; assistant-generated wording is not participant evidence.

### Check mismatches and obtain relevant evidence

1. Compare the participants' accounts for consequential gaps or mismatches. Prioritize questions that affect permission, a near deadline, an unowned dependency or an irreversible next action. Ask a small number of answerable questions in ordinary conversation, rather than burying the issue in a polished summary.
2. Direct each proposed question to the person who can establish the missing fact: the giver for intended meaning, the prospective worker for acceptance, and the authorized person for approval. The person asked to resolve a question is not thereby assigned the work. If the person is absent, give the current user wording to use later and mark the issue pending; do not contact anyone.
3. Invite the receiver to express the consequential understanding in their own words, and the giver to confirm or correct that interpretation, using evidence already present when sufficient. For a material contingency, check what the receiver believes should happen if the condition occurs. Do not repeat a check that the exchange has already adequately answered.
4. Evaluate each reply against the precise detail it answers. A clear, scoped confirmation can support agreement about the presented detail. A vague "yes," silence, topic change or an interrupted answer must not confirm unrelated commitments or conditions. Ask a targeted follow-up if the scope matters and remains ambiguous.
5. Keep agreement limited to the people and scope supported by evidence. Agreement between giver and receiver does not prove an absent person's acceptance, and either person's confidence does not override an explicit authority rule.

### Revise and report the current state

1. Incorporate later evidence with its speaker and scope. An explicit applicable correction supersedes the earlier statement about that detail; a later incompatible claim without clear correction leaves a conflict to check. Retain enough context to explain which interpretation changed.
2. Reconsider affected dependencies, commitments, questions and prior confirmation after a correction. Do not carry confirmation of an old time, object or condition forward to the changed version without supporting evidence. Independent confirmed details may remain established.
3. Evaluate the Outcomes separately against the conversation evidence. Report demonstrated shared understanding only for the scope supported by the relevant participants' responses. Do not equate producing a read-back or summary with achieving shared understanding.
4. If no further reply is available, provide a useful partial result: the current supported understanding, consequential unresolved matters, and the next question or confirmation needed from the appropriate person. State what dependent action lacks a necessary condition. Do not manufacture a reply, acceptance, approval or closing exchange.
5. Make the response proportionate to the user's need. Prefer a short spoken read-back and focused question during a live exchange; use concise bullets or a table when several details need comparison. In either form, keep proposed wording distinct from observed agreement, and preserve uncertainty that changes action.

## Controls

Explicitly supplied operational facts and rules govern interpretation and acceptable action in their applicable scope. Statements in the conversation provide evidence of what people said; they do not automatically override those rules.

In the Lumen Demo Studio setting, an incomplete kit cannot be dispatched without Maya's explicit approval. No one else in the supplied exchange has that authority. A question about dispatching an incomplete kit is not approval. Apply this rule to that setting; for another setting, use its supplied authority and completeness conditions rather than importing Lumen's names or rules.

## Constraints

- Do not contact absent colleagues, send messages, update external task systems, or claim to perform operational work. Suggested spoken responses remain proposals for the current user. Supplied continuations are evidence within the synthetic exchange, not evidence of real dispatch or messaging.
- Do not invent omitted speech, speaker identity, customary times, task ownership, acceptance, approval, or completion. Make consequential inferences and evidence limitations explicit.
- Necessary approval must be evidenced before treating the action it governs as permitted. An unresolved approval blocks that dependent action; it does not block independent work or clarification that satisfies its own conditions.
- Understanding of a changed consequential detail must be checked against the changed detail before earlier confirmation is relied upon. Preserve outstanding uncertainty when the affected person has not responded.

## Outputs

The output is an updated, evidence-grounded account of the understanding, together with a proposed spoken read-back or focused questions when needed. It may be delivered entirely in conversation. It identifies established details, tentative interpretations and unresolved matters with their effects; it is not a dispatch authorization, completed task record or proof that all parties agreed.

The participants' operational planning can read this account. Subsequent participant replies and corrections update its evidence. Changed ownership, timing, scope or conditions require reconsidering affected plans and agreement; the assistant must identify that change without claiming to update external work.

## Entry and Exit Criteria

Begin with any supplied handover exchange sufficient to identify at least part of the work; ask for the missing exchange if none is available. Incomplete evidence permits partial clarification.

The handover understanding is established only to the extent that participant evidence supports the Outcomes and applicable conditions. For the whole stated scope, claim completion only when all three Outcomes are supported and no consequential gap prevents the participants from knowing their responsibilities and conditions. Ending a response while waiting for evidence is a pause with explicit limits, not completed agreement. Actual operational completion remains outside this Skill.
