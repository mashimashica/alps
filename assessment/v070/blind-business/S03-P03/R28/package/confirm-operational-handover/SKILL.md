# Confirm an operational handover

Help the current user state what the available exchange supports, expose consequential uncertainty, and seek confirmation from the person who can resolve it. Treat the conversation, optional speaker-attributed excerpts, and explicitly supplied operational facts as evidence. Preserve speaker order and distinguish quoted commitments from interpretation.

## Build the current understanding

Track each operational item separately. Capture, when supported:

- the work or decision;
- its object or scope;
- the person who accepted responsibility for doing it;
- deadline, handoff point, dependencies, and completion condition;
- whether it is proposed, accepted, confirmed by the relevant person, corrected, completed, refused, or still unresolved;
- the evidence and who supplied it;
- any required authority or approval.

Use cautious status labels that match the evidence. A request, suggestion, prediction, or “might” does not assign work. Silence does not accept it. Asking a question does not make the asker the work owner. A person's read-back shows their understanding; it becomes shared confirmation only when the relevant person affirms it or provides an equivalent clear response. A statement that work will be done is not evidence that it was completed.

Resolve references such as “that one,” relative times, pronouns, and “the usual” only when the conversational context or supplied facts make the referent dependable. Otherwise preserve the ambiguity and say what alternatives matter. When recording, speaker identity, or transcript coverage is uncertain, identify which conclusions inherit that limitation.

Apply corrections to the live understanding: replace superseded details, retain a brief note of the change when it affects action, and re-check downstream implications. Prefer the most recent clear statement from an appropriate source, while surfacing direct conflicts rather than silently choosing one.

## Find consequential gaps

Prioritize gaps that could change what someone does next: an unaccepted owner, conflicting or relative deadlines, missing prerequisite, unclear scope, unresolved exception, absent decision-maker, or proposed action that requires approval. Check explicit operational facts for authority boundaries. Do not treat the most available person as authorized.

Separate:

- **work owner**: the person who accepted responsibility for the task;
- **question owner**: the person expected to obtain or relay an answer;
- **decision authority**: the person whose approval is required.

If someone is absent, record their proposed involvement as pending unless reliable evidence already shows acceptance. Do not contact them or imply they were contacted.

## Respond in ordinary conversation

Choose a natural spoken read-back or a concise structured response based on the user's setting. Include:

1. what appears agreed, with owners and exact times or conditions where known;
2. what remains tentative, disputed, or unconfirmed;
3. the smallest set of direct questions needed before the next consequential action.

Address each question to the person with the knowledge or authority to answer it when that person is present. Phrase questions so a reply can confirm or correct a concrete proposition, for example: “Can you confirm that I own both packing tasks and that reception is the 15:10 deadline?” Ask separately for an exception that needs explicit approval. Do not bundle unrelated uncertainties when separate answers may differ.

If the user is one participant, offer words they can say. If a needed person is absent, identify what must be checked with them and give the user a draft question if useful, without claiming it was sent. Do not invent closure when no reply is available. Instead provide the best current understanding, label its limits, and state which action should wait for confirmation when authority or a critical dependency requires it.

## Incorporate replies

Treat later replies, interruptions, and corrections as new evidence. Attribute each response, update only the propositions it actually resolves, and leave unrelated gaps open. When a response is vague, test the specific unresolved point rather than counting the response as blanket confirmation. Give the revised understanding and, if useful, one final focused read-back.

Never claim to have contacted a colleague, updated a task system, completed operational work, or exercised approval authority. Do not convert the exchange into a fixed form unless the user requests one.
