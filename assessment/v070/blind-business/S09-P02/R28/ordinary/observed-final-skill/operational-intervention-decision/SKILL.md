# Operational intervention decision

Read the supplied measurement definitions and decision context before computing. Treat the packet as authority; identify missing assumptions instead of substituting external benchmarks. Produce a recommendation for the named decision owner and planning horizon, with inspectable calculations. Do not alter operations, records, staffing or spending.

1. Inventory units, date ranges, intervention start, row grain, denominators, outcome windows, recording changes, concurrent interventions and planning constraints. Check duplicate keys, missing rows and impossible counts. Separate measured outcomes from process proxies. Never treat immature outcomes as zero failures or add intercepted defects to downstream failures without a valid definition.
2. Calculate downstream rates with eligible, fully observed denominators. Show aggregate and meaningful subgroup rates, subgroup shares and absolute counts. Retain recent incomplete cohorts for immediately observable outcomes only. Distinguish matched ordinary shifts from atypical shifts. Aggregate each source at its native grain before joining; do not multiply whole-shift labor by subgroup rows.
3. Diagnose comparability. Standardize subgroup rates to a common mix, preferably the next-period mix, and compare contemporaneous untreated operations when available. Show before/after and comparator-adjusted changes separately. State why nonrandom assignment, baseline differences, small samples and concurrent changes limit causality. A difference-in-differences estimate is a sensitivity scenario unless its identifying assumptions are defensible.
4. Calculate throughput, hours per order and service failures on appropriate shifts. Explain labor subsets and strip only documented one-off costs from recurring estimates. Project hours at planned volume, state linear-scaling and mix assumptions, compare with hard staffing limits, and calculate volume feasible at those limits. If subgroup labor is unavailable, say that mix-specific capacity cannot be identified. Check service commitments separately from quality.
5. Show cost scenarios with explicit counterfactual, workload, horizon, unit error cost and incremental recurring hours. Compare gross avoided costs with incremental labor; separate startup costs. Do not price service failures without authority. Include a conservative comparator-based scenario and a favorable scenario where attribution is uncertain. Make the break-even benefit visible. A favorable cost scenario cannot override infeasible staffing.
6. Choose continue, narrow, expand or stop; explain material tradeoffs and rejected alternatives. An untested selective variant needs a bounded trial, not claimed measured savings. Convert the choice into next-period work: scope, staffing ceiling, service response, owner and dated or relative review point. If even the counterfactual has little capacity headroom, flag it and make prelaunch feasibility verification concrete.
7. Specify targeted follow-up only where it could change the choice: data to collect, comparison design, maturity delay, review date and what runs meanwhile. Separate observations, conditional projections and causal claims. Preserve the decision owner's authority.

Deliver a concise decision memo plus a local numerical basis (script, workbook or documented executable commands) that reads unchanged inputs and exposes formulas, intermediate totals, denominators and scenario assumptions. Document invocation and dependencies. Verify the numerical basis locally and disclose checks not performed. Keep case-specific recommendations outside this reusable Skill.
