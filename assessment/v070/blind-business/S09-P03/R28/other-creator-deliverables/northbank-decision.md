# Northbank next-period decision

## Recommendation

For the next four-week period, **stop East's current all-order second-person check and do not expand it to West**. The measured check improved East's mature downstream mispack rate, but East missed the site's 1% loading-cutoff promise during every ordinary pilot shift, and its ongoing labor use was already close to the 84-hour full-shift limit at a lower volume than planned. The packet does not establish that the quality improvement came from the checker alone or that the same labor would support 1,200 orders per shift.

This is a recommendation for Mara Venn's operating decision. It does not change staffing, records, or live systems.

## What the packet establishes

The outcome denominator is mature orders. June 26 has 400 shipped orders per line but zero mature orders, so it is included in dispatch and labor context and excluded from downstream mispack rates. The complete arithmetic is in [`numerical_basis.md`](reproduction/numerical_basis.md), generated by the reusable skill script.

### Mature downstream outcome

| Period | Line | Standard | Complex | All mature orders |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Baseline | East | 36/1,800 = 2.000% | 96/1,200 = 8.000% | 132/3,000 = 4.400% |
| Pilot | East | 27/2,700 = 1.000% | 18/300 = 6.000% | 45/3,000 = 1.500% |
| Baseline | West | 36/1,800 = 2.000% | 84/1,200 = 7.000% | 120/3,000 = 4.000% |
| Pilot | West | 40/2,700 = 1.481% | 19/300 = 6.333% | 59/3,000 = 1.967% |

The aggregate East improvement (4.400% to 1.500%) is not a clean checker result: the mix changed from 60% standard / 40% complex in baseline to 90% / 10% among mature pilot orders, and the common packing-list template changed at the same time. East volunteered for the trial and was not randomized.

Using West as a descriptive comparator and preserving the band split, the estimated incremental reduction is:

```text
standard: (2.000% - 1.000%) - (2.000% - 1.481%) = 0.481481 percentage points
complex:  (8.000% - 6.000%) - (7.000% - 6.333%) = 1.333333 percentage points
planned 80/20 mix: 0.8 * 0.481481% + 0.2 * 1.333333% = 0.651852 percentage points
```

This is a planning estimate, not a causal finding. The complex pilot cell has only 300 mature orders, and the template, account pause, line choice, and checker changed together. Station catches are not added to these errors: recording became mandatory on June 12, so their increase is process context rather than a comparable outcome.

### Dispatch and workload

For the three ordinary full pilot shifts (June 15–17), East had 59 late orders out of 3,000 (1.967%), above the 1% promise. Including the short June 26 shift, East had 63/3,400 (1.853%). Baseline East was 15/3,000 (0.500%). West was 22/3,000 (0.733%) on the ordinary pilot shifts and 24/3,400 (0.706%) including June 26. No authorized dollar value exists for missed cutoffs, so this is reported as an operating constraint rather than monetized.

On the ordinary full shifts, East used 238 productive hours across three days, including four one-off training hours. Ongoing work was `(238 - 4) / 3 = 78.000 hours per shift`, versus 69.000 baseline hours. The measured ongoing increase is therefore 9 hours per shift. The pilot handled 1,000 orders per shift; the next plan is 1,200 orders per shift with an 84-hour limit. There is no measured scaling relationship showing that the check can absorb the extra 200 orders while staying under that limit. The first pilot day reached 82 productive hours including training.

## Workload and cost view for the planned period

The planning packet specifies 20 shifts × 1,200 orders = 24,000 orders per line, an 80% / 20% standard/complex mix, USD 65 per avoidable downstream mispack, and USD 28 per additional productive hour.

If the descriptive band-adjusted estimate were applied to that plan:

```text
quality estimate: 24,000 × 0.00651852 = 156.444 fewer downstream mispacks
avoidable-cost estimate: 156.444 × USD 65 = USD 10,168.89
ongoing labor: 20 × (78 - 69) = 180 additional hours
labor cost: 180 × USD 28 = USD 5,040
quality cost less measured labor delta: USD 10,168.89 - USD 5,040 = USD 5,128.89
```

The last figure is a sensitivity calculation, not an ROI claim. It assumes the nonrandomized estimate transfers to the planned mix, treats all 180 hours as incremental, and assigns no value to the dispatch breach (29 late orders above a 1% allowance in the three ordinary pilot shifts). Those assumptions cannot justify continuing a process that already breached the hard dispatch commitment in the measured full-shift window.

## Bounded follow-up

After stopping the current all-order check, the manager may separately authorize a **five-full-shift feasibility trial of a complex-order-only check**. This variant is unmeasured; the figures above do not claim its savings or capacity. The trial should use the ordinary planned volume and mix as available, keep East at or below 84 productive hours per full shift with no West staff, and use at most the authorized four one-off training hours before launch. Review late dispatches daily and review mature seven-day mispack outcomes seven calendar days after the fifth shift. The decision question is: *at the planned mix, does checking complex orders reduce complex downstream errors while keeping late dispatches at or below 1% and productive hours at or below 84?*

The interim disposition remains the stopped all-order check. If the staffing plan cannot meet the stated cap, do not launch the selective trial; collect ordinary baseline measurements instead. A future continuation or expansion requires the review to show both service feasibility and a decision-relevant quality signal.

## Reproduction

From the task directory, the numerical basis was generated with:

```bash
python3 deliverables/skills/intervention-decision/scripts/analyze_packet.py \
  --sources input/sources \
  --output deliverables/reproduction/numerical_basis.md \
  --next-shifts 20 --orders-per-shift 1200 \
  --standard-share 0.8 --complex-share 0.2 \
  --avoidable-cost 65 --hour-cost 28 \
  --full-shift-min-orders 1000
```

The script validates required columns and integer fields, uses mature orders for outcome rates, aggregates line operations once per date, and writes the cohort, operations, full-shift, and planning tables. It does not infer causality from the records.

## Limits

The packet has no independent estimate of the common template's effect, no randomized assignment, no full-shift observation at the planned 1,200-order volume, and no mature outcome for June 26. It supplies no dollar value for late dispatches. These limits are material to the next-period disposition.
