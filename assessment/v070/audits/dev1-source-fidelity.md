# Source-fidelity review: dev1-001 through dev1-008

## Conclusion

The relevant frozen ALPS guidance is present and mutually consistent on the dimensions examined. The outputs apply it unevenly. They often preserve important work-specific conditions, distinguish processing evidence from business achievement, and allocate useful responsibilities to agents and existing tools. Those strengths coexist with a recurring omission of the separately presented Process core, some changed or incompletely implemented conditions, and uneven verification evidence.

Seven artifacts do not present an Outcome section or a functionally equivalent, separately assessable list of result conditions. Four of those also lack a separate body Purpose section or its equivalent. This is **not** a finding that seven artifacts lack outcome semantics: their workflows and reporting requirements contain substantial, sometimes precise success criteria. Nor is it a requirement to reproduce ALPS headings throughout a business output. The narrower finding is that discovery summaries, action instructions, report-field lists, and embedded success tests have been used in place of the specific separate core required for a Markdown Process Description. The one artifact with that explicit core, dev1-006, still has problems with Outcome separation, scope preservation, implementation correspondence, and evidence claims.

The clearest non-presentation examples are the wrong approval-field comparison in dev1-003; the omitted missing-key path and contradictory verification account in dev1-006; and incomplete propagation of affected evidence into receiving-line judgments in dev1-007 and dev1-008. These are applications of guidance that exists in the sources, not evidence that the sources lack rules about conditions, interfaces, or evaluation.

No numerical business grades, experimental classifications, or causal claims about the creators' behavior are made here.

## What the sources actually require

1. **A semantic core, with a particular Markdown correspondence.** PF §2, lines 27 and 37, requires Name, Purpose, and distinguishable observable Outcomes that are individually necessary and collectively sufficient. PF line 39 distinguishes actions and produced information from result conditions. PF §9, line 151, requires a title and separate Purpose and Outcomes sections or equivalents, with one or more Outcome list items. PD lines 33 and 39, and AWS line 43, explicitly carry those requirements into target Skills. This is not inferred from a template, validator, or preferred visual style.
2. **Necessary work detail, not exhaustive decomposition.** PF §3 allows optional elements and omission of unhelpful levels; Tasks must make action, object, and force clear. PF line 153 recommends Activities with numbered Tasks, but permits Tasks without grouping where grouping adds no useful distinction. An explicit Procedure can carry necessary order. Therefore a useful short procedure is credited; lack of Activity headings is not independently treated as a defect.
3. **Functional roles, not a heading checklist.** PF §4 classifies information by its occurrence and use. A tool supplies capability; a receipt is produced evidence and later an input to interpretation; a matching rule is a Control; authorization and retry restrictions are Constraints. One resource may have several identifiable roles. Mixed headings can still communicate those roles. AWS lines 33 and 34 require the configuration to relate capabilities, tool operations, information, criteria, and restrictions without equating Task boundaries with tool interfaces.
4. **Scope-preserving conditions and usable interfaces.** PF §§4 and 7 and DP §§4 and 5 require conditions to limit the dependent work, preserve the applicable source meaning, and communicate invocation, returned information, limitations, failures, incomplete results, and uncertain state effects. AWS lines 27, 44, and 45 require preserved work meaning, available resources, and conditions confirmed before dependent operations.
5. **Separate evidence subjects.** PF §8, DP §6, AWS lines 49, 53, and 55, and AG's verification section distinguish description validity, component/connection behavior, and effective representative work. A format pass, executable script, or honestly disclosed omission is not evidence that an untested condition is satisfied. AWS line 63 expressly says unperformed evaluation remains unconfirmed.

The responsibilities do not conflict: PF governs the description and shared work meaning; DP governs the system that realizes it. Direct CLI reuse is expressly allowed by AWS line 42. Neither source demands a wrapper, extra documents, one tool per Task, a separate Process per component, or ALPS labels on every business report.

SC's advice to keep instructions concise, avoid arbitrary structure, and preserve real invariants does not waive the applicable PF requirements. It also says to state the desired outcome and validate observable behavior, and warns that its format validator does not prove good decisions. I found no instruction in this frozen material that authorizes replacing an independently assessable result core with discovery metadata or treating unperformed tests as passed. The observations below establish inconsistent application; they do not establish whether retrieval, interpretation, prioritization, or another cause produced it.

## Artifact findings

### dev1-001

**Core and work detail.** The title names the work. Its frontmatter and operational instructions convey release intent, but the body opens with tool use and “supports release decisions” rather than a separate high-level Purpose. There is no separate Outcome list. This does not erase its strong result semantics: SKILL line 64 requires the intended production candidate/digest, health, and checkout success, and lines 71 and 75 distinguish supported requirements and a correctly blocked release from production success. The “Result reporting” bullets specify assessment content, not a separately stated list of result conditions. The Procedure is usable and makes qualification, approval, promotion, and post-promotion checking dependencies explicit; another Activity hierarchy is not necessary just for conformity.

**Roles, conditions, and interface.** Inputs and authority, command arguments, JSON evidence, state effects, and assessment outputs are functionally identifiable without separate Enabler/Control headings. Reusing the supplied CLI is appropriate. SKILL line 33 nevertheless mandates qualification “even if an older qualification exists.” In `input/release_tool.py`, qualification creates a new sequence at line 24 and promotion checks approval against that exact sequence at line 36. Thus this unconditional requalification changes the approval basis even when current matching evidence may already exist. The brief requires qualification before exact approval, but does not require replacing valid qualification on every application. This is an added method/condition that narrows applicability without identifying its rationale or consequences, rather than missing source guidance about scope. The later independent-work/authorization distinction at SKILL line 75 is otherwise well preserved.

**Evidence limit.** The note reports a format-validator pass, not a release or recovery trial. Its explicit non-execution limits are faithful to PF §8 and DP §6. They leave effectiveness unconfirmed; they do not cure the core or applicability findings.

### dev1-002

**Core and work detail.** Name and a separate Purpose are clear. The four Activities and their imperative Tasks are useful functional groupings. SKILL lines 44 and 45 independently describe production identity, health, and checkout checks, while line 46 states the combined criterion for calling release achieved. This is meaningful success guidance, not merely a list of commands. However, there is no separate Outcome list or equivalent. “Outputs” at line 63 describes the release assessment product, and does not replace that core.

**Roles, conditions, and interface.** The operation reference and JSON/exit-status guidance clearly relate CLI capability to evidence and agent judgment. Line 61's blanket description of candidate, digest, sequence, owner identity, and request ID as “release controls” is imprecise: values are also input/evidence, and the matching rules and approval criteria supply the controlling role. The surrounding Tasks largely recover the functional distinction, so this is not wholesale absence of information roles. The mandatory `qualify` at line 32 has the same unqualified renewal problem established from this trial's own supplied tool, lines 24 and 36: the described work does not explain why existing valid qualification must be replaced or account for the resulting need for approval of the new sequence. Missing approval is otherwise scoped to promotion, and uncertain effects receive an explicit status/inspection path.

**Evidence limit.** The note reports structural inspection only, expressly no frozen-validator run and no release instance. This is a limited and appropriately qualified evidence account, not proof of complete description validity or component behavior.

### dev1-003

**Core and work detail.** The Purpose is explicit; Activities distinguish basis, qualification/promotion, and uncertain-effect/result handling. SKILL line 71 contains concrete success conditions, and reporting distinguishes observations, requirements, uncertainty, and follow-up. Those semantics deserve credit. As in the preceding artifact, no separate Outcome list or equivalent is provided.

**Conditions and interface correspondence.** SKILL line 35 says approval's `qualification_sequence` must be later than the covered qualification sequence. The supplied tool instead requires `approval.qualification_sequence == qualification.sequence`; it separately requires `approval.sequence > qualification.sequence` (`input/release_tool.py`, line 36). This is an actual field/criterion mismatch, not a terminology preference. SKILL line 87 gives the later-approval rule generically but does not correct the earlier wrong field. A user following both statements lacks a coherent approval test. DP's interface-information requirement and PF's criterion coherence apply directly.

SKILL line 14 also lists a request ID specifically “for promotion,” then requires stopping before **all** state-changing operations if any required input is absent. Qualification itself changes state, so this broad instruction can block otherwise authorized qualification solely because a promotion-specific input is missing. The brief explicitly permits qualification independently of missing promotion approval, and the foundations require uncertainty to limit dependent work. The narrower continuation language elsewhere does not fully resolve this overbroad instruction. Conditional requalification at line 39 is appropriately more context-sensitive than a universal renewal rule; no extra hierarchy is needed to express it.

**Evidence limit.** The note reports source reading and authoring inspection, but no business operation or formal format validation. Those limitations are explicit. The static interface contradiction remains despite the reported reading of the tool.

### dev1-004

**Core and work detail.** The separate Purpose covers intended candidate availability and production evidence. The Tasks preserve exact-candidate approval, condition-scoped qualification, and distinct health/checkout assessment. SKILL lines 62, 63, and 64 provide real result criteria, and line 76 correctly says a safety stop is not a successful release. They are still embedded in work instructions rather than a separate Outcome list. The creator explicitly describes “Outcomes expressed through the workflow's success criteria” in the note, line 12. That is evidence of the chosen presentation, not independent confirmation that PF §9 was satisfied.

**Roles, conditions, and interface.** The interface description distinguishes observation, evidence recording, promotion effects, and assessment. Conditional requalification at line 54 preserves useful choice and the dependencies remain explicit. Line 58 overstates evidence when it permits a “matching production state” to be treated as the effect of the original request. A matching revision supports current-state judgment; without the corresponding request record it does not, by itself, establish which request caused it. The supplied tool exposes request-specific evidence separately (`input/release_tool.py`, lines 12 and 40). DP §4 and PF §8 require this distinction in the interpretation and scope of evidence. The same line's same-key/status-first retry direction is otherwise a substantive strength.

**Evidence limit.** The note reports manual file/frontmatter/link inspection and clearly leaves promotion, recovery, and failure scenarios untested. It expressly names the Agent Work System Skill and both foundations, but does not expressly report reading the Process Description Design Skill. That is an uncertainty about the reading account, not proof that it was not read.

### dev1-005

**Core and work detail.** The Name, frontmatter, and operational prose identify recoverable booking. SKILL line 32 states meaningful confirmed/rejected/unresolved criteria: persisted matching receipt, persisted rejection reason, and remaining reconciliation. These are semantic result conditions even though the body supplies neither a separate Purpose nor an Outcome list. “Operations” gives a compact invocation-oriented procedure; its usefulness is not defeated by lack of Activity headings. Necessary handling detail is missing, however, for a nonterminal lookup response and for preservation of recovery information under overlapping calls.

**Roles and allocation.** Line 34 makes an important correct distinction: the ledger is recovery information, while the simulator's committed receipt is booking truth. The wrapper supplies a justified capability missing from the bare service interface: recording generated identity/payload before attempting a reservation. `scripts/book.py`, lines 42, 46, and 47, substantiate that intended allocation. The simulator remains unchanged, and the agent is instructed not to infer a booking from availability or retry with a fresh key. No alternative architecture is required by the foundations.

**Interface and realization gaps.** In `scripts/book.py`, lines 36, 38, and 40, a successful `lookup` response is returned with exit 0 even when it is `not_seen`. SKILL line 32 explains only the three business dispositions and supplies no explicit mapping of this fourth service observation to unresolved or a safe same-payload retry. This is precisely the incomplete-result/next-action information required by DP §4, not a missing cosmetic section. The ledger is loaded once at script line 28 and whole-file-replaced at lines 47 and 50 without serialization. Overlapping operations can read the same prior ledger and overwrite another operation's recovery record; atomic replacement alone does not make the read-modify-write interaction atomic. This is a static realization risk to the explicit concurrency and post-process recovery conditions in the brief, not a runtime result from this review. SQLite's reservation boundary does not protect the wrapper's separate ledger.

The claim at SKILL line 34 that only the named database and ledger change is also too narrow for its own implementation: script lines 7 and 8 create directories/temporary files, and the supplied interface describes a possible SQLite journal sidecar. These are local ancillary effects, not unauthorized external bookings, but the effect contract should not exclude them.

**Evidence limit.** Reported normal reservation, replay, post-process reconciliation, and compilation are relevant but do not cover the admitted omissions of fault-mode recovery and concurrency. The note correctly keeps those omissions explicit; it does not establish the corresponding design conditions as met.

### dev1-006

**Core and work detail.** This artifact supplies a title, separate Purpose, and listed Outcomes. The conditional confirmed/rejected/unresolved statements are sensible for the brief's alternative dispositions; they need not all occur in one booking. Still, Outcome line 17 combines two independently assessable invariants—no overselling and no duplicated successful request—and line 15 joins persisted rejection evidence with the distinct no-new-authorization retry condition. PF §2 requires independently assessable results to remain distinguishable. The Purpose's “terminal result” including “unresolved” at line 10 is also terminologically inconsistent with lines 30 and 35, which correctly leave reconciliation open. “Unresolved” can be a truthful reported disposition without being a terminal booking receipt.

The two Activities are useful and concise. “Inputs” at line 21 mixes the processing tool with request/state information; the tool's actual role is an Enabler. Subsequent invocation and journal/evidence distinctions make its function reasonably recoverable, so this is a role-label precision issue rather than an absent system boundary.

**Preserved and changed conditions.** Lookup-first recovery and exact-payload retry are documented and implemented (`scripts/reconcile_booking.py`, lines 43 and 45); the no-availability-as-confirmation and local-only conditions are explicit. But SKILL line 27 says to refuse to invent a key, and the adapter makes `--request-key` mandatory at line 31. The brief, line 5, expressly requires the supporting system to establish and make a missing key recoverable before attempting a reservation. No other delivered capability performs that work. This removes an in-scope path without a declared or justified change. A general prohibition on invention has displaced a specific authorized responsibility.

**Interface and realization gaps.** SKILL lines 36 and 52 promise distinguishable conflict/invalid-input handling. The adapter's post-call logic, lines 46, 48, and 49, converts a simulator error object that is not a terminal receipt into generic unresolved/exit 75; in particular, a remote idempotency conflict not already present in the local journal loses its error identity. Local journal conflicts are handled at line 36, which does not cover this distinct connection response. The load/whole-file-save design at lines 34, 39, and 47 also lacks serialization across overlapping journal updates. The simulator's transaction does not protect that separate caller state. These are DP interface/connection and necessary-condition issues, not reasons to demand a particular wrapper architecture.

**Contradictory evidence claim.** SKILL line 56 says the wrapper was checked for rejection and both pre-commit and post-commit fault recovery. The note lists only normal confirmation, replay, and recover, and expressly says fault-mode trials were not completed at line 14. The rejection check is not substantiated by the note; the fault-check claim directly contradicts it. Which checks actually occurred cannot be independently determined here. The delivered assertion therefore cannot support those judgments and violates the sources' requirement that unperformed or unconfirmed evaluation remain explicit. This is not fixed by the presence of an Outcomes heading.

### dev1-007

**Core and semantic equivalents.** The supplied brief already gives a Name, Purpose, and three independently assessable Outcomes. The delivered title preserves the Name. Its frontmatter, five workflow Tasks, and response contract substantially preserve the three intended results: supported line positions, recipient/action follow-up, and explicit evidence gaps. SKILL lines 17, 18, and 22 make these distinguishable in use. It would therefore be wrong to say the work meaning disappeared merely because ALPS headings disappeared. Nevertheless, the body supplies no separate Purpose or listed Outcome core or a reference carrying that core; the “Response format” is an Output-content instruction. The concise Task list is a useful level of work description, without a need to add Activity grouping.

**Roles, conditions, and interface.** The agent/processor boundary is particularly clear at line 12: arithmetic/anomaly processing supplies evidence, while the agent interprets it and drafts follow-up. Coverage is treated as evidence, not scope removal; incomplete/conflicting evidence limits final comparison; recipients must come from supplied information and drafts must not be sent. These are meaningful functional mappings despite the absence of element-name sections.

The implemented conflict handling does not completely deliver that promise. `scripts/review_receiving.py`, lines 34 and 35, records a differing repeat only by event ID and immediately skips its content. Line 47 attaches a conflict only to lines containing the retained event. If the other content names another in-scope line, that second affected line need not be blocked and its association is absent from the global conflict detail. The brief requires identifying affected lines. This is a concrete information-supply/criterion-propagation gap under DP §4 and AWS line 33. An agent could reconstruct the missing association from raw input, but the documented processor result alone does not supply it. This is not a claim that a future agent necessarily fails.

At line 15 the script is called “read-only,” while line 26 and script line 55 correctly describe optional output-file writes. The narrower meaning—no receiving-record mutation—is clear elsewhere, but the unqualified label is inconsistent with the interface's actual local effects. The nested input schema is also not documented or linked in the Skill; the top-level field list and bundled code expose some, but not all, operational meaning. A self-contained contract should not depend on guessing the omitted responsibility-map structure.

**Evidence limit.** The note reports a shortfall/incomplete/out-of-scope component example, compilation, and format validation, while explicitly leaving several other branches and effective future agent interpretation unestablished. This separation is source-faithful. It also leaves the identified conflict branch without reported supporting verification.

### dev1-008

**Core and semantic equivalents.** As in this trial's supplied brief, receipt position, concrete recipient-specific follow-up, and explicit gaps are the work's independent concerns. They remain materially present in SKILL lines 8, 24, 26, 27, and 31. There is no separate Purpose or Outcome list in the body, and the instruction to return a review is not itself proof that those conditions are achieved. This is an omitted separate core, not wholesale loss of meaning. The short agent procedure gives a useful analysis-to-follow-up structure; adding a second grouping layer is not necessary.

**Roles and interface dependencies.** The processor prepares evidence and deterministic comparisons; the agent checks source plausibility, interprets limits, and drafts without sending. The body expressly warns against treating the processor as proof of achieved outcomes. This preserves the foundations' responsibility boundary. However, line 12 relies on a schema “as described in the work brief,” and no such brief or uniquely identified runtime reference is packaged in this output. The processor exposes field names in code, but that is not a complete documented source/condition of use. AWS line 44, PD line 43, and PF §6 already provide the relevant guidance.

**Conditions not fully propagated into results.** `scripts/review_receipts.py`, line 39, puts unknown-SKU events into a separate identity list; lines 45, 46, and 47 never use that list to limit affected order-line judgments. Thus a current-month identity anomaly can coexist with a final line classification even though the supplied brief requires identity reconciliation before final comparisons. Line 39 also collects unknown-SKU events before the month filter at line 40, losing the brief's current-month limitation for that identity finding. Different-content duplicate IDs are recorded at line 35 but skipped at line 36, while affected-line conflict status is attached through retained events only at line 46; a second affected line can again be missed. These are scoped-evidence and interface/criterion discrepancies visible in the code. The agent may be able to repair interpretation using the supplied input and global findings, but neither that behavior nor sufficient documented reconciliation is established here.

**Evidence limit.** The note reports compilation and a no-argument usage-error check only. They support syntax and that invocation-error branch, not arithmetic, completeness, identity, conflict, or end-to-end follow-up behavior. The note's final limitations appropriately avoid a future-effectiveness claim. Absence of a supplied business instance does not turn those unperformed component checks into evidence of correctness.

## Source sufficiency and assessment limits

The requested guidance is not absent: the Process core and atomic Outcomes are explicit in PF §2 and §9 and both design Skills; proportional granularity and functional roles are explicit in PF §§3 and 4; dependency-scoped conditions, source preservation, interface failures, information provenance, and uncertain effects are explicit in both foundations; and the distinction between description, component, and work-system evidence is repeated in AG, PF, DP, and AWS.

The artifacts demonstrate partial uptake of these rules. In particular, direct CLI reuse, useful deterministic processing, truthful uncertainty, local-only authorization, and the distinction between processing evidence and achieved work recur in the delivered text. The gaps concern how that guidance was realized: the separate core was commonly not retained; some source conditions were narrowed or mistranslated; some tool responses did not supply or propagate the needed uncertainty/identity; and one delivered verification claim conflicts with its own note.

The review does not require all business outputs to look like ALPS, does not infer conformity from familiar headings, and does not convert every missing optional section into a finding. Conversely, useful prose and honest limits do not by themselves satisfy a missing mandatory core or an unmet condition. The evidence supports source-fidelity findings about these specific artifacts only. It does not independently establish actual business outcomes, comprehensive program correctness, model capability, future system effectiveness, or the causal reason for inconsistent application.
