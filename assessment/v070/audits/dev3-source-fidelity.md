# Static source-fidelity review: fidelity-dev3

This review concerns the six packaged Skills in fidelity-dev3 against their own original business inputs. I read every complete target SKILL.md, both receiving scripts, all four supplied release_tool.py files, and F03's bundled interface reference. The criteria are the frozen ALPS AGENTS.md, Process Description Design and its Process Framework, and Agent Work System Design and its design principles. Relevant local examples were consulted as informative material. No evaluated artifact was changed, business instance executed, or consumer evidence consulted. Identifiers below identify packet cases only.

The Framework requires a Name, Purpose, and observable Outcomes, with independently assessable results distinguishable and collectively sufficient for the purpose. Its Markdown core structure is mandatory, while additional element sections and activity grouping depend on usefulness. The system principles require faithful allocation, sufficient information and interfaces, and visible failure and uncertainty. This review consequently distinguishes missing presentation, missing or altered business meaning, and implementation contradictions. An instruction elsewhere in a Skill counts as retained meaning; a section title or completed calculation does not establish its promised result.

F19, F27, and F61 have titles but no separate Purpose and Outcomes sections or equivalent result lists. That violates the Framework's specified Markdown correspondence, rather than proving that all underlying business meaning disappeared. F19's Release procedure, step 5, explicitly requires the intended candidate deployed, successful health, and successful checkout; it also says a blocked promotion has not achieved the intended release. F61's Judge production outcome requires healthy production and completed checkout for the same promoted revision. F27's interpretation tasks require scope confirmation, evidence-limited judgments, and concrete recipient-specific next actions. These conditions remain individually assessable through their prose. F27's opening emphasizes producing a summary and drafting, however, so the coordinator's ability to direct remaining follow-up is less clearly stated as the organizing objective.

F03 and F80 demonstrate a different defect: having Outcome headings does not supply sufficient Outcomes. F03's fourth item accepts confirmed promotion or correct withholding; its fifth requires health and checkout observations to be recorded and judged. With valid qualification and approval, a confirmed promotion followed by recorded, correctly diagnosed checkout failure can satisfy every listed item without delivering working checkout. F03's assessment task 3 correctly says such failure leaves release success unmet, preserving the business safeguard but exposing an inconsistent semantic center. F80's Outcomes similarly describe inspection records, gated attempts, resolved effects, probing, and reporting. They can be satisfied while production is unhealthy. Its procedure step 6 supplies the missing operational success test, and reporting explicitly distinguishes a correct stop from achievement. These are sufficiency and action/result distinctions, not demands for a preferred number of bullets. The two production results must remain distinguishable; more administrative milestones cannot replace them.

F42's Purpose closely preserves its original objective. Its Outcomes concern supported line judgments, actionable follow-up, and visible evidence limitations, rather than merely generating JSON. Recipient and next action together characterize usable follow-up; splitting them into additional Outcomes would not improve this review. Its Activities cover establishing evidence and interpreting/directing work. F27 describes essentially the same necessary work without that organization. Both retain the prohibition on sending messages or changing receiving records and assign final interpretation and drafting to the agent. The principal receiving defects therefore concern realization of retained requirements, not wholesale loss of the original work.

For release approval, every supplied release_tool.py requires both approval.qualification_sequence equal to qualification.sequence and approval.sequence strictly greater than qualification.sequence. F61's SKILL line 26 instead requires the approval to have “a qualification sequence later than the recorded qualification sequence.” That confuses the qualification being approved with the time of approval and conflicts with the exact-qualification requirement. F80 repeats the same problem in Inputs and controls, line 21, while procedure step 4 explicitly states the correct equality and inequality. Its correct detail mitigates ambiguity but does not erase the contradictory normative statement. F03's exact-event Purpose and F19's requirement that approval cover “this exact qualification” preserve the business relationship, although F03's compressed Controls sentence would benefit from unambiguous field correspondence. The tool's gate limits possible effects; it does not make contradictory agent instructions faithful.

All four release workflows instruct qualification before their approval check, even where inspected state may already contain valid current qualification and matching approval. F80 explicitly says to qualify even if older qualification exists. In the supplied tool, every qualification increments sequence and replaces the qualification record; existing approval then fails its exact-sequence match. No approval operation is exposed by this command. An already usable basis can therefore become unusable until a fresh owner action occurs. The brief requires qualification followed by approval, but does not universally require rerunning an already applicable qualification. This is an avoidable method restriction and feasibility risk, not evidence that any particular release was blocked. The descriptions should distinguish necessary requalification from reuse of confirmed current evidence and identify the owner dependency without inventing approval.

F27's review_receiving.py contradicts its Interface claim that conflicting event content affects every referenced in-scope line. At line 32, only the currently encountered key is added to conflicts; the first record's key is not recovered. If one event ID first identifies line A and later identifies line B, A can retain a final comparison while B is marked conflicting. At line 36, an unknown SKU produces an order identity issue before month filtering, which occurs only at line 43. Thus an older-month unknown SKU can block current-month line judgments, although the source limits this order-wide reconciliation condition to current-month events. Conversely, a missing event_month can be silently filtered out without an invalid-event diagnostic. These are different failures: incomplete conflict coverage, overbroad blocking, and unnoticed missing evidence.

F27 also promises event evidence in each returned line, but line 54 returns aggregate quantities and counts without event IDs or records. The agent is instructed to inspect that evidence although it must reconstruct it from the input. Invalid order quantities are reported as issues and skipped rather than represented as unresolved line records. Invalid-evidence positions can have neither recipient nor next action. The agent's scope and drafting obligations provide a possible correction path, but the supplied deterministic summary does not itself fulfill its advertised support for complete, traceable line review.

F42's review_receipts.py recognizes a current-month absent SKU and appends an identity_mismatch issue at line 49, but never propagates that issue into the affected order's line evidence or comparison gate. Lines can consequently receive complete shortfall, excess, or received-as-ordered classifications when the original description requires identity reconciliation first. Conflicting event variants are discarded at line 39; conflict_keys is subsequently built only from retained first records. Later referenced lines can escape conflict marking. For a marked line, adding conflict evidence at line 55 causes the incomplete-evidence branch to run; the subsequent conflict-specific branch at line 60 is unreachable. Routing therefore asks the data steward for a full export rather than directing the purchasing coordinator and data steward to reconcile conflicting records. These conclusions follow directly from control flow and do not depend on executing a test case.

F42 also changes the scope of uncertainty handling. SKILL line 50 says no line judgment should be complete on invalid input, while the script's final status becomes invalid_input for any accumulated validation error. A defective record can therefore prevent final judgments on unrelated lines with sound evidence. The source explicitly preserves such evidence, so this blanket restriction requires correction even though it avoids overstating success. Missing recipients can also silently remove entries from follow_ups because line 69 appends only when a recipient exists. The Skill's exit conditions still require every unresolved line to have a recipient and drafted action; the agent must expose the gap rather than read an absent entry as no necessary follow-up.

Information roles are generally recoverable without extra headings. Requests, state, orders, events, and coverage declarations supply examined information; approval rules, comparison rules, and completeness conditions govern judgments; simulation-only and draft-only restrictions limit execution. Existing commands and bundled scripts provide capability. F03 lists the command among Inputs but separately explains it under Enablers; that is not sufficient evidence of operational role confusion. Using existing release commands directly is appropriate, and their absence from the packages is not a missing bundled dependency because the briefs explicitly supply them per application. The receiving scripts appropriately combine settled processing into one operation, but their rules and diagnostics must actually preserve the source conditions. Packaged script and F03 reference targets were available.

These are static description and code findings. They do not establish the frequency of agent errors, actual release or receiving results, or comparative effectiveness. Both receiving Skills reserve final judgment for the agent; that allocation may permit recovery but does not prove recovery occurred or cure incorrect component evidence. F03 explicitly limits representative checks to exercised cases, and F42 distinguishes component checks from future agent effectiveness. No claim here rests on an artifact's existence, command completion, or inferred test history. Component execution, full system trials, and external physical-format specification checks were not performed. The material correction priorities are faithful success conditions, consistent approval predicates, and receiving evidence propagation and routing; adding optional sections alone would not address them.
