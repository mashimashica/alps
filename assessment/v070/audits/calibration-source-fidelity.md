# Calibration source-fidelity review

## Conclusion and scope

The six submissions show variable application of the frozen ALPS guidance. **cal-001, cal-003, cal-007, and cal-011 substantially preserve its required Process core and relevant distinctions. cal-005 and cal-009 preserve substantial operational meaning but do not fully apply that core.** Concrete implementation and condition-scope issues are distinguishable from those presentation/content omissions. None establishes that the corresponding ALPS rule is absent or defective.

This is a source-fidelity review, not a replacement business grade or a claim of successful execution. Reviewed only the six permitted briefs, complete output Skill resources, and execution notes, plus the frozen ALPS sources below. No grades, other trials, mappings, candidate-B1, PR/history, or improvement hypotheses were consulted. No evaluated file was changed. Execution notes are author reports, not independently verified command logs. Scripts and packaged tests were read, not executed; the release simulator inputs were not read. Findings about behavior below are static source observations, not new forward-test results.

## Applied source and normative force

- [AGENTS.md](../frozen/alps/AGENTS.md), “Sources” and “Verification and delivery,” separates the two foundations, informative drafting aids, physical checks, semantic review, and work-system effectiveness.
- [Process Framework](../frozen/alps/skills/design-process-description/references/process-framework.md) (PF), §§1–4, 6, 8–9: Name, Purpose, and Outcomes are mandatory; Markdown requires separate Purpose and Outcomes sections **or semantic equivalents**. Optional elements are driven by necessary detail. Activity grouping is recommended, with an ungrouped Tasks section expressly allowed. Information roles are functional, not inferred from labels. Required conditions constrain dependent work, not unrelated work. Outputs, requirements satisfaction, and achieved Outcomes remain distinct.
- [Process Description Design](../frozen/alps/skills/design-process-description/SKILL.md), “Success and work description” and “Controls,” explicitly applies PF to Agent Skill bodies and requires independently assessable success conditions and necessary work detail.
- [Agent Work System Design](../frozen/alps/skills/design-agent-work-system/SKILL.md), “Configuring responsibilities and interactions,” “Realizing the requested configuration,” and “Evaluating and adjusting the system,” requires preservation of the work, identifiable tool/criterion/constraint relationships, necessary interfaces and conditions, and scoped verification.
- [Design Principles for Agent Work Systems](../frozen/alps/skills/design-agent-work-system/references/agent-work-system-design.md) (WS), §§2–6, supplies the allocation, information, authority, uncertain-effect, and evaluation requirements.

The [minimal template](../frozen/alps/skills/design-process-description/references/SKILL-template.md), both Skills’ reference examples, and the [working example](../frozen/alps/examples/README.md), including its target description and tool instructions, were also read. They are informative: they neither require every demonstrated section nor excuse omission of the mandatory core. These authoring sources need not become runtime dependencies of each self-contained target Skill.

## Concrete submission-level gaps

### 1. Missing declared core is real, but does not prove business inadequacy

PF §2 (line 27) and §9 (line 151), and Process Description Design’s Agent Skill correspondence (line 39), explicitly require the core in the body. cal-005 and cal-009 both have names and discovery summaries; neither has a separate purpose account and listed result-condition section, under those names or equivalents. The finding therefore is not a fixed-string check for `## Outcomes`.

Both Skills nevertheless communicate important success conditions through their actions and reporting requirements. Their omissions do **not** establish that an agent cannot perform the work, that all success semantics are absent, or that longer text would improve effectiveness. Conversely, discovering implicit conditions in instructions does not satisfy the source’s explicit requirement for a declared, separately assessable Process core.

### 2. cal-005’s processor does not fully preserve its own evidence rules

In [monthly_review.py](../trials/cal-005/output/receiving-review/scripts/monthly_review.py):

- Line 42 marks a line affected by a conflicting event when either its `order_id` **or** its `sku` matches. Thus conflicting variants for `PO-1/A` can mark an otherwise sound `PO-2/A` conflicting merely because the SKU is shared. This exceeds the affected-line relationship in the [brief](../trials/cal-005/input/brief.md) and the Skill’s instruction to leave unrelated sound lines usable.
- Lines 44–45 consult only the first matching coverage declaration. Opposed completeness declarations are not reconciled before the status branch can issue a final receipt comparison. This does not preserve the requirement for confirmed, valid evidence behind a dependent final judgment.

These are substantive correspondence findings, not deductions from script length. PF §4 and WS §5 already require scoped conditions and explicit uncertainty; work-system design also requires implemented rules to retain their source and scope. The target agent is told to examine evidence and input errors, so these observations do not by themselves establish every agent-mediated outcome. They establish that a processor status cannot be trusted as a faithful implementation across these conditions.

The Skill’s input description also defers nested contract details to “the request” (line 12), without packaging that request as a reference. The script exposes field names, but the Skill does not provide a complete user-facing nested contract or explicitly direct the reader to recover it from code. That is a self-contained interface-information gap under WS §4, not a requirement for a particular documentation filename.

### 3. cal-009 broadens prerequisites and leaves operational authority underspecified

The [Skill](../trials/cal-009/output/checkout-release/SKILL.md), line 8, permits use “only” when a promotion request ID is supplied, although inspection, qualification, and assessment do not use that ID. The [brief](../trials/cal-009/input/brief.md) expressly permits preparatory work without promotion approval; PF §4 and WS §5 scope each unmet condition to the actions depending on it. Requiring a stable identifier for promotion/retry is faithful; turning it into a prerequisite for the entire Skill is an unjustified broader restriction.

Procedure step 3 also commands qualification without a condition covering already usable recorded qualification, and the target does not explicitly identify qualification as a write requiring per-request operational authorization. Its later exact-qualification approval rule means a new qualification cannot simply inherit approval for a different qualification. The brief requires authorization per simulation application; supplying paths or possessing service-owner approval is not that separate authorization. These are supporting-configuration omissions/extra restrictions, not reasons to reject procedural presentation. Simulator-specific effects were not independently verified in this review.

## Evaluation limits and implications for ALPS

The six execution notes—[cal-001](../trials/cal-001/execution-note.md), [cal-003](../trials/cal-003/execution-note.md), [cal-005](../trials/cal-005/execution-note.md), [cal-007](../trials/cal-007/execution-note.md), [cal-009](../trials/cal-009/execution-note.md), and [cal-011](../trials/cal-011/execution-note.md)—distinguish authoring/checking from a completed business application. cal-007 and cal-011 report richer component/connection checks and explicitly leave intended-agent end-to-end effectiveness unconfirmed. cal-009 reports no operational check. These are different evidence limits, not automatic success or failure of the work. The reported inability to retrieve a live format reference or run a particular validator is not evidence that Process meaning failed; physical-format assurance is a separate subject.

The clear source-level requirements already address the established gaps: declared Purpose/Outcomes, faithful implemented rules, sufficient interfaces, scoped prerequisites, and authorization before dependent effects. The reviewed examples also demonstrate both minimal and richer targets without making their optional sections compulsory. The evidence supports **inconsistent uptake and local realization**, not a causal finding that ALPS teaches the wrong behavior. Whether a source change would improve uptake requires separate evidence; neither extra headings/word count nor these six outputs alone establishes that improvement.
