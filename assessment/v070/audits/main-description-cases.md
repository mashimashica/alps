# Intended discrimination of the three main briefs

These are independent, original synthetic business descriptions for creating Skills, not Skills themselves. The visible examples establish the business setting and public evidence contract. They are not hidden consumer inputs, expected transcripts, gold answers or an exhaustive grading oracle. No concrete hidden consumer cases have been authored here. The descriptions do not depend on external services or on private facts about the fictional organizations.

## S02 — One workshop has to move

The central distinction is whether a Skill can support a bounded, one-off decision using local constraints without turning the work into a permanent operating system or pretending that advice executed a move.

- **Normal:** Complete, consistent room and route information permits useful comparison and a decision that respects the coordinator's authority.
- **Boundary:** A room can look attractive while failing an access, capacity, timing or total-cost requirement; an apparently viable alternative can remain unreserved. These differences should matter.
- **Incomplete:** Missing availability, access, equipment or authority evidence can justify a focused question, a conditional recommendation, or a conclusion that confirmation is not yet possible.
- **Changed context:** A later closure, amended requirement or updated availability can invalidate earlier advice. Revising or withdrawing that advice is a legitimate result.

Several forms of help can be good: a brief conversational recommendation, an evidence-backed comparison, a conditional next move, or a concise explanation that the available options do not work. No specific room, writing format or sequence is required by this audit. A consumer exercise can observe use of the supplied facts and claimed action status, but cannot establish a real booking, physical accessibility or attendee receipt of a notice.

## S03 — Make sure the handover was understood

The distinction is between shared-understanding support and merely producing a plausible-looking recap or completed handover document. Important evidence lies in who said what, what was corrected, what was accepted and what remains unanswered.

- **Normal:** Clear dialogue can support a short read-back and a grounded account of the understanding reached.
- **Boundary:** Tentative offers, absent colleagues, ambiguous references and different operational deadlines must not be flattened into confirmed ownership or agreement.
- **Incomplete:** An unfinished exchange can legitimately end with a targeted question and an explicitly partial understanding. Lack of closure is not itself failure.
- **Changed context:** A later correction or reply can alter the understanding or resolve a gap without requiring the whole exchange to start again.

Legitimate outcomes include a spoken-style response, clarification addressed to the present user, a compact statement of the current interpretation, or an explicit inability to confirm a consequential point. Different wording and question order may work. The exercise can observe conversational claims and responses, not a person's unexpressed comprehension, an absent person's agreement or completion of physical kit work. Speaker and transcription uncertainty limit what can responsibly be concluded.

## S04 — Work out what the proof-request pilot can support

The distinction is whether a Skill can support repeated clarification and feasibility work while maintaining shared information within its permissions, and can separately answer a cross-cutting inquiry without changing that information.

- **Normal:** Grounded discussions can refine requirements and the corresponding working records while retaining relevant agreement, uncertainty and feasibility evidence.
- **Boundary:** Stakeholder desire, operational commitment and technical capability have different owners. A request for an overview is not authorization to edit, even when a discrepancy is visible.
- **Incomplete:** Missing effort evidence, vague terms or unresolved disagreement can support questions, conditional options or a deferred decision rather than an invented estimate or consensus.
- **Changed context:** New evidence or an authorized revised decision can affect several work areas. Shared notes and later overviews should remain distinguishable from superseded information.

Legitimate outcomes include clarification, a scoped update to existing notes, alternative scope options, a read-only synthesis, or a reasoned conclusion that feasibility is still unknown. No prescribed labels, universal workflow, document schema or preferred feature set is necessary. Supplied edit permissions and actual local changes can make the read/write distinction observable; the exercise cannot prove production-system feasibility, stakeholder adoption or the accuracy of an unsupported effort estimate.

## Limits across the set

The cases intentionally differ in duration, evidence form and authorized activity. They should not reward a particular length, heading structure or terminology. S02 and S03 require no persistent working artifact; S04 involves supplied shared information but does not require a fixed new artifact. Necessary clarification, honest deferral and refusal to imply unauthorized external action are potentially successful business outcomes. Any later evaluation must judge the actual supplied consumer evidence and permissions, not secretly add organizational customs, capabilities or authority rules.
