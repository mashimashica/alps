# Recommendation to Nessa Vale: stop Alder's all-order packing check for the next 20 full shifts

This is a recommendation; Nessa retains the operating decision. For the next 20 full shifts, stop Alder's second-person check before sealing and run both lines without that added check, while keeping the centrally issued packing-list template and mandatory catch scanning already in force. Do not expand the check to Birch or substitute a selective checking scheme during this horizon.

The all-order check is infeasible under the authorized plan and did not show a downstream quality benefit. On ordinary pilot shifts, Alder's ongoing labor projects proportionally to **108.0 hours per 1,440-order shift**, exceeding the hard **102-hour** cap by **6.0 hours**. Its late-dispatch rate was **42/3,600 = 1.17%**, above the **1%** commercial commitment. At the decision mix, downstream mispacks were **3.7% on Alder versus 2.5% on Birch** during the pilot. The estimated intervention benefit is negative under both reasonable observational contrasts, and both sampling intervals include zero.

## Workload, dispatch, capacity, and cost

The next-period plan is 20 shifts × 1,440 orders = **28,800 orders per line**, at 70% standard / 30% complex.

| Check | Observed or projected result | Requirement | Decision implication |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Alder pilot ongoing labor, ordinary shifts | 90.0 h per 1,200 orders; 108.0 h projected at 1,440 | ≤102 h/shift | All-order continuation is infeasible by 6.0 h/shift. The one-off five training hours were excluded. |
| Alder pilot dispatch, ordinary shifts | 42 / 3,600 = 1.17% late | ≤1.00% over the period | Extrapolation gives 336 late orders versus at most 288; 48 above the commitment. |
| Birch pilot labor, no checker | 82.0 h per 1,200; 98.4 h projected | ≤102 h/shift | Current no-check operation has 3.6 h projected headroom. |
| Birch pilot dispatch, no checker | 18 / 3,600 = 0.50% late | ≤1.00% over the period | Extrapolation gives 144 late orders, within the commitment. |

The labor projection assumes constant hours per order between 1,200 and 1,440 orders; it is a planning scenario, not proven capacity. Using the contemporaneous and difference-in-differences labor contrasts, all-order checking adds an estimated **7.2–9.6 hours per planned shift**, or **144–192 hours** over 20 shifts, costing **$4,896–$6,528** at the authorized $34/hour. No separate dollar ceiling is supplied; the hard labor cap blocks the option regardless of that cost estimate.

At $55 per downstream mispack, the two quality point estimates imply **$9,504–$19,008 in additional avoidable error cost**, based on the 28,800-order workload and full attribution of the descriptive contrasts to checking. This is a sensitivity range, not a causal forecast. Missed cutoff consequences remain unpriced, as required.

## Why the other scopes fail

- **Continue all-order checking on Alder:** eliminated by the labor cap; observed pilot dispatch also exceeded the commitment, and measured quality did not improve.
- **Expand all-order checking to Birch:** unsupported by the quality evidence and projected to require about 105.6–108.0 hours per shift if the measured incremental workload transfers, above the cap.
- **Check only standard orders:** unmeasured and, under equal effort per order, projects to 103.44–105.12 hours per shift, above the cap. The observed standard-order quality contrast was zero.
- **Check only complex orders:** an equal-effort assumption projects 100.56–101.28 hours per shift, nominally below the cap by only 0.72–1.44 hours. The records contain no checking time by band, complex work may take longer, and selective checking has never been measured. Its observed quality direction was adverse: Alder's pilot complex rate was 10% versus Birch's 6%. This is too fragile to authorize for the 20-shift horizon.

## What the packet establishes, suggests, and cannot establish

**Establishes:** the mature outcome counts above; August 28 outcomes are not mature; ordinary pilot Alder dispatch was above 1%; proportional all-order labor exceeds the hard cap; and no selective scope has measured time, quality, or dispatch performance.

**Suggests:** all-order checking added 7.2–9.6 labor hours per planned shift and did not reduce downstream mispacks. A no-check operation is the only supplied operating pattern with projected headroom and observed dispatch compliance under comparable ordinary shifts.

**Cannot establish:** that checking caused Alder's worse mix-adjusted result; whether a selective check would improve quality or reliably fit; actual full-shift capacity at 1,440 orders; or the effect of the packing-list template independent of checking.

No further evidence is necessary to decide this 20-shift horizon because the measured all-order variant breaches a hard constraint and the unmeasured selective variants lack a positive quality basis. Any later reconsideration should require a separately authorized, timed selective-check design that remains at or below 102 hours, specifies ordinary-shift exposure and dispatch limits in advance, waits seven calendar days after its last shipment for outcomes to mature, and keeps the no-check process as the interim default.
