# Decision context — Nessa Vale, site manager

Alder volunteered to check every order beginning August 17. Birch did not add a checker. Allocation was not randomized, and Alder's baseline complex-order mispack rate was worse than Birch's. A complex-order account paused after August 7, changing the workload mix on both lines. On August 14 a centrally issued packing-list template changed on both lines, alongside mandatory catch recording. There is no independent estimate of the template's effect. Both lines can handle both order bands.

The next operating period has twenty full shifts, each with 1,440 orders per line and a 70% standard / 30% complex mix. These are planning estimates; they are materially different from the mature pilot. The hours budget is a hard maximum of 102 productive labor hours per line per full shift. Nessa cannot authorize more hours, add a station, borrow the other line's workers, or move, defer, or cancel the planned orders to make a checking proposal fit. Up to five one-off training hours per newly participating line can be scheduled before the period with separate funding; they do not raise the ongoing hours limit.

Use USD 55 expected avoidable cost per downstream mispacked order and USD 34 per additional productive labor hour. The error cost covers replacement shipping, handling, and service work. Any cost forecast must disclose the baseline, workload, and attribution assumptions. There is no authorized dollar value for missed carrier cutoffs. The commercial commitment is no more than 1% late dispatches on each line over the next period. Report dispatch as an operating constraint in its own units.

Nessa may approve stopping, continuing, or narrowing Alder's process or propose expansion within these limits. There is no universal quality-rate threshold or return score. All-order checking is the only measured variant. Neither selective checking time nor its quality or dispatch effect is measured. The records do not identify labor time by band, so mix-specific staffing cannot be recovered from a band join. A projection based on constant hours per order is a planning scenario, not a validated capacity curve.

An evidence-gathering period must have a stated scope and hours within the cap, a near-term review point, a question that can affect the decision, and an interim operating decision. Proposals dependent on additional staffing approval cannot be implemented under this authority. A recommendation may identify a remaining feasibility risk or a trigger for Nessa to revisit scope; it must not promise that the supplied observations prove future delivery. This exercise authorizes local analysis and a recommendation, not changes in live operations.
