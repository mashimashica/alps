# One workshop has to move

Create an Agent Skill that helps a coordinator resolve the relocation of one scheduled workshop. The Skill will be used later by a fresh assistant with the relevant local information; this assignment is to design that help, not to relocate anything now.

At the fictional Northbank Design Co-operative, Jules is coordinating an 18-person workshop on 16 October 2030, from 09:30 to 15:30. Water damage has made the original room unusable. The sponsor wants to retain the date, length and planned activities if possible. Jules needs to know whether a workable move exists, what it would change for the people involved, and what remains to be agreed before calling the move confirmed. This is a one-off disruption, not a request for an events-management system.

The public scenario facts are:

- The group needs seats and tables for 18, a step-free entrance and route, an HDMI display, a surface on which removable workshop notes are permitted, and access from 09:00 to 16:00 for setup and clearing away.
- All participants will initially arrive at Northbank reception. Any alternative must be reachable from there by a step-free route of no more than ten minutes. The supplied route notes, not a map search, establish those journeys.
- Jules may spend at most £180 in total on the replacement room and required equipment. Only Jules can authorize a move or any spending. The sponsor must approve a change to the workshop date, length or activities.
- An internal room note lists Alder: 20 seats, 09:00–16:00, £145 including the required display and note surface, with a documented eight-minute step-free route. Cedar has 24 seats and costs £110 but is reached only by stairs. Willow costs £90 and has a step-free route, but its stated seated capacity is 16. These are availability observations, not reservations.
- Reception can redirect arriving participants once Jules has agreed the change. No participant contact details are needed to assess the move.

Help should leave Jules with a defensible decision or an honest explanation of why one cannot yet be made. Practical consequences and outstanding confirmations matter as much as comparing rooms. A plausible room must not become a claimed booking, and a constraint must not quietly disappear because it is inconvenient. If the supplied facts change, the advice must reflect the change rather than defend an earlier suggestion.

For later use, the consumer will receive a small local evidence bundle: dated room and route notes, the workshop's stated needs, the relevant approval boundaries, and any subsequent messages supplied as text. Exact paths will be provided. The bundle is synthetic and read-only; it may contain missing information or newer corrections. No room service, email account or live calendar is available or required. The assistant may prepare wording for Jules to use, but must not book a room, spend money, send messages or imply those actions occurred. No particular report, template or document is required.
