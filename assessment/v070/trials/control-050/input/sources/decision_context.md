# Decision context — authorized by site manager Mara Venn

The trial is a second-person check of the packed units and accessory list before sealing. It began on East on June 15. West did not add a checker. Neither line was randomized; East volunteered and its baseline complex-order mispack rate was worse than West's. Both lines can pack both order bands.

There were two other relevant changes. A large complex-order account paused shipments after June 10, changing both lines' mix. A clearer centrally supplied packing-list template went live on both lines on June 12, together with the station-catch recording change. The manager has no independent estimate of the template's effect. Dates June 1–3 and June 15–17 are ordinary Monday–Wednesday full shifts, without promotions, equipment outages, or bank holidays. The much smaller June 26 shift should not be treated as an ordinary full shift merely because it is more recent.

The next four-week plan is twenty full shifts. East is expected to ship 1,200 orders per shift with an 80% standard / 20% complex mix. West is also planned at 1,200 per shift with that mix. The volume and mix are planning estimates, not guaranteed outcomes. The next-period budget allows at most 84 productive labor hours per line per full shift, with no extra packing station and no borrowing West staff to rescue East. The site manager cannot authorize more hours in this decision. Up to four one-off training hours per newly participating line could be scheduled before the period; those are separately funded and do not increase the ongoing 84-hour limit.

For a planning estimate, use an expected avoidable cost of USD 65 per downstream mispacked order. It covers the typical replacement shipment, handling, and customer service work; it is not revenue or a contractual fine. Each additional productive labor hour costs USD 28 on average. The finance partner asks that cost reasoning make its baseline and workload assumptions visible, and that it not claim all before/after change was caused by the checker. There is no authorized dollar value for a missed dispatch cutoff; report its operational effect separately instead of inventing one.

The commercial promise is that no more than 1% of orders miss the loading cutoff over the next operating period. The manager also cares about downstream quality, staff workload, and feasibility at the planned mix and volume. The 1% dispatch commitment is not a substitute for evaluating quality, nor is a lower aggregate mispack percentage sufficient to justify expansion. There is no universal mispack-rate threshold or mandatory return-on-investment score.

The decision owner can approve continuing, stopping, or narrowing the process on East, or propose expansion subject to the same limits. An evidence-gathering period must have a defined scope, staffing within the stated budget, a near-term review point, and a decision-relevant question. A selective version of the check has not yet been measured: the current trial checks every East order. Do not present savings or capacity for an untested variant as observed fact.

This packet supports a local recommendation, not instructions to change live systems. The manager retains the actual operating decision.
