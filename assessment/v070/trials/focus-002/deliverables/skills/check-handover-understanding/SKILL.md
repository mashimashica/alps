# Handover Understanding Check

## Purpose

Make the degree of shared operational understanding visible and correctable so that people handing work over can rely on the same owners, timing, remaining actions, dependencies, and decision limits without concealing uncertainty.

This work interprets and checks the supplied exchange. It does not perform the operational work, contact absent people, or grant approval.

## Outcomes

- The current understanding identifies each consequential handover item's subject, action or status, owner, timing, dependencies, constraints, and decision authority to the extent supported by the available evidence.
- Each item's agreement state is explicit and traceable to the supplied speakers and order, with statements, acceptances, read-backs, proposals, questions, corrections, and confirmations kept distinguishable.
- Conflicts, vague references, missing coverage, unanswered matters, and uncertain speaker attribution remain visible with their operational effects.
- Each material uncertainty identifies the person or authority able to resolve it and a focused check that could obtain the missing evidence.
- Later replies and corrections update every affected item without turning silence or an earlier superseded statement into closure.

## Activities & Tasks

The following Tasks are required. Revisit affected Tasks when later evidence changes the understanding.

### Establish the evidence

1. Read the supplied conversation or speaker-attributed excerpts in order together with any explicitly supplied operational facts. Record any stated limit in speaker identity, recording reliability, or coverage that weakens the dependent interpretation.
2. Separate consequential items at a granularity that a participant could accept or correct independently. For each item, determine what the evidence supplies about the subject, action or current status, work owner, time or place, dependency, constraint, and decision authority.
3. Resolve phrases such as “that one,” relative times, or role references only when the supplied context identifies a unique meaning. Otherwise retain the phrase and state the plausible ambiguity and its effect.
4. Treat a later explicit correction as superseding the earlier statement within the corrected scope. Keep the change and its source visible, and reconsider every item that depended on the earlier statement.
5. Keep contradictions separate until the relevant person resolves them. Do not silently combine them into a smooth summary.

### Judge what is shared

1. Distinguish, in language suited to the exchange, at least these evidence states when they occur:
   - a direct statement or commitment by the person concerned;
   - an explicit acceptance by the proposed work owner;
   - compatible statements from different participants that indicate the same understanding but have not been explicitly confirmed;
   - a read-back that shows only the reader's current understanding;
   - a proposal, request, conditional idea, or unanswered question;
   - an explicit confirmation or correction by the person whose understanding, commitment, or authority is at issue;
   - an unresolved conflict, ambiguity, or missing response; and
   - a superseded statement retained only as history.
2. Count ownership as established only to the degree supported by an assignment made with applicable authority, the proposed owner's acceptance, or the owner's own commitment. Do not assign work to an absent person from a possibility, request, or unanswered message.
3. Record the owner of a question separately from the owner of the work or decision it concerns. Direct an exception or waiver question to the stated authority; do not infer authority from participation, convenience, or silence.
4. Keep planned, assigned, started, completed, blocked, and approved states separate. A promise to act, a question about acting, or an approval request is not evidence that the action occurred.

### Check and revise the understanding

1. Give a concise conversational read-back of what the evidence currently supports. Make commitments and current timing easy to scan, and pair each material uncertainty with its consequence. Use a compact list or table only when it makes the exchange easier to check; no fixed handover form is required.
2. Ask the fewest focused questions that could change a consequential interpretation. Address a present participant directly when they can answer. When the needed person is absent, formulate the question and identify who needs to answer without claiming to contact them.
3. Request explicit confirmation for the particular items whose shared understanding matters. Treat a reply as evidence only for the scope it clearly addresses; a read-back alone is not confirmation by another person.
4. Incorporate later confirmations, corrections, interruptions, and newly supplied facts. Update the current view, mark displaced information as superseded, and reopen any dependent conclusion whose evidence changed.
5. If the exchange ends without another reply, provide the best supported current view and clearly identify open items, who can resolve them, and which dependent actions remain blocked. Do not manufacture agreement or completion. Independent work may remain actionable when its own conditions are satisfied.

## Inputs

- A conversation or ordered, speaker-attributed transcript or excerpt.
- Optional local plain-text excerpts and explicitly supplied operational facts, including authority or timing constraints.

## Outputs

- A current evidence-based view of the handover understanding, expressed in ordinary conversation or another format useful to the user.
- Focused confirmation or correction questions and, when replies are unavailable, an explicit account of the remaining uncertainty and its effects.

## Controls

Use the supplied conversation, its speaker order, explicit operational facts, and applicable authority constraints as the basis for interpretation. The newest applicable explicit correction controls the corrected point while its prior form remains identifiable as superseded.

## Constraints

- Do not claim that another person confirmed a read-back unless that person actually supplied a confirming response.
- Do not turn a suggestion, question, request, or silence into an acceptance, commitment, approval, or completed action.
- Do not contact an absent colleague, perform the handed-over work, or claim that an operational action occurred.
- An absent or unconfirmed approval prevents only the action governed by that approval; it does not itself prevent unrelated work whose conditions are satisfied.

## Exit Criteria

Each consequential item is either supported at the stated agreement level or explicitly unresolved with its effect and necessary source of resolution. Any claimed confirmation, completion, or approval is backed by applicable evidence from the supplied exchange or facts.
