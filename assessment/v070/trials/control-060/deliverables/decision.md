## What happens during the next period

Recommend the no-routine-checker process on East and unchanged West practice, both using the current template, for the period. No new spending, training, station, or staffing transfer is assumed. The manager should reserve **at most 84 productive hours per line each full shift**, including all operational monitoring time. If they cannot make a credible schedule within the cap, the manager must resolve that capacity gap within existing resources before relying on the shipment promise. This recommendation supplies no authority to increase hours or reduce customer commitments.
2. **Shifts 1–3:** run without the routine checker and review order counts/mix, paid hours, hourly completion progress, and late orders at each shift close. The question is whether removing checking restores sufficient dispatch capacity at the actual planned workload. Monitor West as well. Any forecast beyond 84 hours or cumulative site lateness above 1% triggers same-day manager review and adjustment of work sequencing within the existing allocation. With 2,400 actual site orders, 1% corresponds to 24; always use actual volume. An early shift's result is a warning against the whole-period promise, not a redefinition of that promise. If performance cannot be recovered, report the unresolved delivery risk immediately; do not restart full checking or assume unauthorized rescue labor.
3. **End of shift 3:** review operational feasibility. If service and workload remain credible, continue the no-checker process through the remaining shifts with daily monitoring. If results fail or are inconclusive, retain no routine checking as the interim resource-conserving action while the manager addresses scheduling within the cap. The packet provides no proven process that guarantees all commitments, and a failed review must not be labeled success.
4. **Seven calendar days after shift 3:** review fully mature standard and complex quality cohorts from those first three shifts, alongside West and the prior packet on common weights. Examine the matured June 26 results separately. This tests whether quality deteriorates enough to reconsider checking, while recognizing that another nonrandom short window cannot isolate causation. Continue to collect later mature outcomes through the period; recent zeros remain unavailable evidence.

A later **complex-only** check could focus attention on the highest-risk band, but the packet has no measured labor, dispatch, or quality performance for it. Even its net capacity benefit cannot be assumed. Do not launch that variant in this recommendation: reconsider only after the manager has evidence of workload headroom, a measured estimate of check time by band, and an explicit bounded scope fitting the same 84-hour limit. Any proposed experiment must include service guardrails and a quality readout after seven days; otherwise retain the current recommendation. Full expansion remains unjustified without measured feasibility and stronger evidence of incremental quality value.
