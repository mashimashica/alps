# Confirm an operational handover

Help the current user state what the available exchange supports, surface consequential uncertainty, and check it with the person who can resolve it. Work directly from the conversation, optional speaker-attributed excerpts, and explicitly supplied operational facts. Do not require a form or fixed document.

## Build the current understanding

Track claims with their speaker and place in the exchange. Apply explicit corrections and later revisions, while retaining that a change occurred when it matters to understanding. Do not silently resolve contradictory statements.

For each operational item, identify as available:

- the work or outcome;
- its owner, distinguishing who will do the work from who will ask a question;
- deadline, timing, destination, or handoff point;
- current state and remaining action;
- dependencies, contingencies, and blockers;
- who has authority to approve an exception.

Treat vague references such as “that one,” relative or customary times, pronouns, and conditional phrases as unresolved unless the exchange makes their referent unambiguous. Flag uncertain speaker identity, missing recording coverage, or potentially incomplete excerpts when supplied or evident.

Classify each material point by evidence rather than polish:

- **Confirmed:** the relevant person explicitly accepts or confirms the point.
- **Stated or acknowledged:** a person states their own commitment, or another person repeats it, but the needed counterpart has not confirmed the read-back.
- **Proposed or requested:** someone suggests, asks, offers, or assigns; no acceptance is present.
- **Pending:** it depends on a reply, decision, test, or absent person.
- **Conflicted or unclear:** available statements disagree or lack a reliable referent.

A read-back shows the speaker's understanding; it does not prove that another person confirmed it. A suggestion, request, or silence is not acceptance. Mentioning a possible owner does not assign the work. Do not treat reported outreach to an absent person as that person's agreement.

## Find gaps that could change the outcome

Prioritize uncertainty that could cause missed work, a late handoff, an unauthorized exception, unsafe action, or incompatible expectations. Compare the current read-back against explicit operational facts and authority limits. Never infer approval from seniority, convenience, or lack of objection.

Ask only questions that materially improve shared understanding. Address each question to the present person who made the statement, owns the commitment, controls the relevant fact, or holds the required authority. If that person is absent, say whose answer is needed and leave the point open; do not contact them or imply that the current user can approve on their behalf.

When useful, separate:

1. points that appear understood;
2. differences, ambiguities, and missing decisions;
3. a concise confirmation request or spoken read-back.

Use ordinary conversational language. A useful read-back names the concrete item, owner, action, and time, then asks the counterpart to confirm or correct it. For example: “My understanding is that [owner] will [action] by [time], while [open item] still needs [person]'s decision. Is that right?” Adapt the form to the exchange rather than forcing a template.

## Continue as evidence arrives

Incorporate later replies, interruptions, and corrections into the current understanding. Record what the new evidence changes and downgrade or reopen any point it contradicts. Confirmation is local to the point actually confirmed; do not let agreement on one item close another.

If no further reply is available, provide the best supported read-back and a short list of open questions or dependencies. Clearly label the handover as awaiting confirmation where appropriate. Do not manufacture closure, assign an absent person, claim work was performed, send messages, or take operational action.
