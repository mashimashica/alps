June 26's 400 orders per line have no mature outcomes as of June 29; their zeros do not represent zero errors. Their seven-day window ends July 3; refresh after it has fully elapsed. Complex share fell from 40% to 10% on both lines following the account pause. East's headline reduction of 2.90 percentage points therefore overstates its improvement at a fixed workload mix. Standardization is `0.8 × standard rate + 0.2 × complex rate`: East improves 1.20 points, West 0.548 points. Subtracting West's improvement leaves 0.652 points as a comparator-adjusted signal. East also has a 0.452-point advantage over contemporary West at the planned mix.

Neither comparison identifies the check's causal effect. Both lines received the clearer template, East volunteered, and East started with worse complex quality. Regression toward typical performance and differential template effects remain plausible. Only three mature pilot full shifts and 300 complex orders per line are observed; one additional complex error changes the standardized rate by 0.067 points. No randomized inference or confidence guarantee is supplied. The data support improvement and a possible incremental benefit, not attributing the entire change to verification.

## Workload and dispatch decide feasibility

Labor is aggregated once per date and line. Overtime and training are subsets of productive hours. Remove East's four one-off training hours only when projecting recurring work.

Projection formula: recurring hours / shipped orders × 1,200. East universal checking exceeds its cap by 9.6 hours per shift, or 192 hours over twenty shifts. Its implied capacity shortfall is about 123 orders each shift, 2,462 over the period. These are linear workload estimates, not an independently demonstrated maximum throughput. June 26 was a short 400-order shift (East 31 hours, West 28); it is not evidence that either line handles a full planned shift within those hours.

No band-specific labor exists. The pilot mix was only 10% complex against a planned 20%, so the 93.6-hour figure cannot be treated as a mix-adjusted forecast; more complex work could worsen capacity. East baseline's 82.8-hour estimate used 40% complex and predates the template; West's current no-check proxy is 83.6 hours at 10% complex. Both imply thin slack, not proof of feasibility after stopping. Removing training already lowers East from 95.2 to 93.6 hours at planned volume; training funding does not solve recurring workload. East overtime rose from 9 to 26 hours across the matched three shifts, consistent with workload pressure.

At pilot late rates and planned volumes, East would have about 472 late orders, West 176: 648/48,000 = 1.35%, above the site's 1% allowance of 480. That extrapolation is conditional, but the matched pilot already missed the site rate (81/6,000 = 1.35%) at lower volume. Including the short shift yields East 63/3,400 = 1.85% and West 24/3,400 = 0.71%; recency does not remove the concern. No dollar penalty is invented for lateness. A hypothetical East baseline late rate plus current West rate gives 296 late orders over the plan, but is not a guarantee after stopping.

## Financial promise is conditional

The following scenarios compare universal East checking against alternative no-check proxies for **the same 24,000 East orders**. Quality rates use the planned mix; labor uses observed whole-line intensity and cannot be mix-adjusted. Avoided errors × $65 gives gross avoidable cost; incremental recurring hours × $28 gives labor cost. Positive net values are expected avoided costs, not revenue or realized savings.

| Counterfactual scenario | Avoided errors | Gross benefit | Additional hours / cost | Net benefit | Break-even avoided errors |

| East baseline, all fixed-mix improvement attributed (favorable) | 288.0 | $18,720 | 216 / $6,048 | $12,672 | 93.0 |

For example, adjusted incremental hours = 24,000 × [(234−207)/3,000 − (209−205)/3,000] = 184. The contemporary scenario needs a 0.359-percentage-point benefit to break even, versus the observed 0.452-point gap: a modest margin sensitive to confounding and labor mix. If the checker has no incremental quality effect, it loses $5,152 under the adjusted labor scenario. The table is a set of sensitivities, not a confidence interval. Existing training is sunk; a new line could require up to four separately funded hours (up to $112 at the stated rate), excluded here. Expansion would still need recurring capacity evidence. These favorable economic scenarios assume work the universal-check configuration cannot deliver within the staffing cap; they do not justify exceeding it.
