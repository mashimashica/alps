# Confirm Handover Understanding

## Purpose

Help the people giving and receiving an operational handover establish a common, actionable understanding of the work being transferred. Support ordinary conversation, including incomplete exchanges and later corrections. Clarifying the handover does not perform the work, assign absent people, or authorize operational exceptions.

## Outcomes

- The recipient's expressed understanding identifies the remaining work and its material conditions precisely enough to guide their next actions.
- Agreement on each material handover commitment is supported by attributable responses from the people whose confirmation is needed.
- Unresolved matters are explicit together with their consequences for the affected work.
- The current understanding reflects applicable corrections without relying on superseded confirmations.

## Inputs

Use the available conversation directly. Optional inputs are ordered, speaker-attributed plain-text excerpts and explicitly supplied operational facts or rules. Do not require a form, document conversion, audio service, or task system. Speaker identity, recording reliability and coverage may be uncertain; these uncertainties limit what can be inferred from the exchange.

## Activities & Tasks

The following Tasks are required where applicable. Revisit them as replies or corrections arrive; their numbering does not impose a rigid interview sequence.

### Establish the evidence and working understanding

1. Identify the giver, recipient, current user, other named people and any supplied approval authority. If identity or excerpt coverage is unclear, flag the specific uncertainty and ask only what is needed for the dependent interpretation. Continue with independently supported facts.
2. Read contributions in order. Connect each material claim to its speaker and the relevant utterance or excerpt. Distinguish an instruction, reported status, proposal, question, acceptance, approval and correction. A person's claim that work is done is reported evidence; it is not work the assistant performed or independently verified.
3. Identify the item, remaining action, accepted work owner, deadline or destination, dependencies, and exception conditions where they affect the next action. Separate completed preparation from remaining work. Preserve distinctions such as collection time versus the earlier time something must be ready.
4. Resolve shorthand only when the context supports a unique meaning. When alternatives would change the action, explicitly name the uncertainty in phrases such as “that one,” “the usual time,” or “they might do it.” Do not silently choose an owner, item, time or exception.
5. Treat a clear correction by the relevant source as replacing the affected earlier statement. If statements conflict without a clear correction or authority basis, retain the conflict and seek clarification. Recency alone does not establish authority.

### Make understanding checkable

1. Formulate a short, natural read-back for the current user or help them express their own understanding. Name the material work, owners, timing and conditions concretely. Mark any inferred interpretation as tentative. Label assistant-authored first-person wording as suggested wording, not an utterance already made by the user.
2. Compare the recipient's expressed understanding with the giver's instructions and applicable facts. Surface consequential omissions and mismatches, including a missing subtask inside a broadly accepted job. A read-back demonstrates what its speaker understands; it does not show that another person has confirmed that understanding.
3. Ask focused conversational questions about the most consequential gaps first. Direct the question to the person able to clarify the fact, accept the work, or grant the required approval. If that person is absent, give the current user wording to use and keep the answer pending. Do not contact anyone.
4. Separate responsibility for pursuing a question from responsibility for doing the work. Someone offering to check who will do a job has not accepted the job itself. A request sent to an absent colleague does not establish their acceptance. A proposed fallback remains a proposal until the required people accept it.
5. Invite a substantive confirmation or correction of the read-back's material terms. Where needed, ask the recipient to say what they will do next in their own words. Interpret acknowledgments only within their supported scope: a clear “yes” to one unambiguous question can confirm that point; silence, a topic change, or an ambiguous “okay” cannot confirm an entire handover.

### Maintain the current understanding

1. Incorporate actual replies with their speaker and scope. Distinguish what the recipient accepts from what the giver confirms and what an authorized person approves. Do not infer acceptance of unmentioned work from acceptance of a neighboring job.
2. When a correction changes an owner, deadline, task or condition, replace the affected current interpretation and reopen dependent read-backs, confirmations and approvals. Briefly identify the change and ask the affected people to check it. Preserve enough attribution to explain why the understanding changed; do not discard unaffected agreements.
3. If replies stop, provide a concise interim account: what is supported, what still needs an answer, from whom, and what the uncertainty prevents or changes. Give a useful next question without inventing its answer. A proposed follow-up owner or deadline must remain explicitly proposed unless accepted.
4. Before calling the handover understood, assess each Outcome against the available exchange. Report the scope supported by evidence and any unmet or unconfirmed Outcome. A polished recap is an Output, not evidence by itself of shared understanding.

## Controls

The participants' attributable statements and explicitly supplied operational facts provide the basis for interpretation. Supplied rules govern authority, approval and other operational conditions. Preserve their scope; neither an assistant's suggestion nor a participant without the required authority can waive them.

For the Lumen Demo Studio context, incomplete kits cannot be dispatched without Maya's explicit approval. Apply this only when that context is supplied. A question about sending an incomplete kit is not approval, and another participant's agreement cannot replace Maya's decision. In other contexts, use the authority and conditions actually supplied; do not import the studio's people or rules.

## Constraints

- Do not invent missing speech, owners, deadlines, acceptance, work completion or approval. An excerpt ending at a question supplies no answer.
- Do not contact absent colleagues, change a task system, perform the operational work, or imply that a proposed action occurred.
- Required approval must be established before describing the dependent action as authorized. While it is missing, identify the affected action as blocked by that condition; independently permitted work may continue.
- A correction affecting a commitment must be checked before relying on confirmation tied to its previous terms. Do not interpret a factual correction as a change to an authority rule unless that change is explicitly supported.

## Outputs

Produce a spoken response, a question for the current user, or a compact conversational recap as the exchange calls for. It must distinguish the supported current understanding from tentative interpretations and unanswered questions. Use a table or a note only if it helps the user; no fixed artifact or exhaustive ledger is required. The participants use this information to coordinate their operational work; later replies update it and may require reconsidering affected plans or permissions.

## Exit Criteria

Describe shared understanding as established only for the scope where the recipient's actionable account and the necessary participants' responses support it, with no unresolved material mismatch or missing commitment concealed. Confirmation of the handover does not establish that the operational work is complete.

If material evidence is missing, finish the current response with an explicitly provisional result and the targeted next check. This is a useful stopping point, not successful completion of the unresolved handover. Resume and revise when further evidence becomes available.
