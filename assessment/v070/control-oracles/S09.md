# S09 control business oracle

These two synthetic consumer packets assess whether a supplied, fixed Skill helps an operator make a usable packing-intervention decision with inspectable arithmetic. They were authored from the frozen S09 brief and its four source files only. No target Skill, generated answer, other case, or earlier oracle was consulted. The reference calculations below are not a required report schema, analysis script, or unique decision. Equivalent calculations and materially justified alternatives are acceptable. Outcome achievement here means producing an evidence-grounded recommendation that the manager can use, not proving that future operations have improved.

## Ordinary: Cedar Quay

Harbor's three mature pilot shifts have the same volume and 75/25 mix as baseline and the next-period plan. Both bands improve; Ridge improves slightly. There is no documented concurrent process or recording change. This supports a favorable local continuation case, with uncertainty from voluntary assignment, few shifts, and transport to future work.

| Mature outcome | Harbor baseline | Harbor pilot | Ridge baseline | Ridge pilot |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Standard errors / orders | 27/1,800 = 1.50% | 12/1,800 = 0.6667% | 27/1,800 = 1.50% | 27/1,800 = 1.50% |
| Complex errors / orders | 36/600 = 6.00% | 18/600 = 3.00% | 36/600 = 6.00% | 33/600 = 5.50% |
| Total errors / orders | 63/2,400 = 2.625% | 30/2,400 = 1.25% | 63/2,400 = 2.625% | 60/2,400 = 2.50% |

These aggregates already use the planned mix. Harbor's raw reduction is 1.375 percentage points; subtracting Ridge's 0.125-point reduction leaves a 1.25-point comparison-adjusted signal. The adjustment is a transparent planning comparison, not proof that checking caused the difference.

| Full-shift operation | Harbor baseline | Harbor pilot | Ridge baseline | Ridge pilot |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Paid productive hours, three shifts | 186 | 198 | 186 | 186 |
| One-off training within those hours | 0 | 3 | 0 | 0 |
| Recurring hours per shift | 62 | 65 | 62 | 62 |
| Late dispatches / shipped orders | 14/2,400 = 0.5833% | 13/2,400 = 0.5417% | 14/2,400 = 0.5833% | 13/2,400 = 0.5417% |

Harbor's 65 recurring hours fit the 68-hour limit at the observed and planned workload, leaving three hours of planning headroom. Overtime is already included: adding its 15 pilot hours would misstate costs. Removing training for recurring forecasts is appropriate; deleting it from historical paid totals is not. The July 31 short shift is not a full-capacity observation. Its 320 orders cannot enter the downstream denominator, but its two Harbor late orders can enter an all-pilot dispatch calculation: 15/2,720 = 0.5515%. Catches are consistently logged here, yet remain pre-dispatch process counts rather than downstream errors.

For 20 × 800 = 16,000 Harbor orders, the comparison-adjusted scenario gives 200 avoided mispacks × $48 = $9,600 avoided cost. Three extra hours × 20 × $32 = $1,920 labor, leaving $7,680 expected net benefit. A disclosed raw before/after scenario gives 220 avoided errors and $8,640 net benefit; presenting this as guaranteed checker savings fails. The adjusted scenario breaks even at 40 avoided errors, or a 0.25-percentage-point reduction, which can inform uncertainty without imposing a universal return threshold.

Continuing all-order Harbor checking for a bounded period is defensible: retain a staffing plan around 65 hours within the cap, review immediate hours and dispatch after the first few full shifts, then examine complete seven-day quality cohorts by band. A useful mature review has an explicit time, for example after five new shipment cohorts have completed their windows. Dispatch exceeding the 1% commitment, hours approaching the cap, or failure to reproduce a useful quality advantage should lead to a specified scope reconsideration. Numerical discretionary triggers need rationale, not false contractual status.

Expansion, narrowing, or stopping can also be acceptable if their tradeoffs and supporting assumptions are credible. Expansion requires an explicit Ridge staffing and three-hour pre-period training plan and acknowledges that Harbor's effect may not transfer. A selective variant has no observed savings or capacity estimate. Generic refusal to decide because randomization is absent misses the useful favorable evidence and feasible bounded option.

## Challenging: Fenwick

Alder's aggregate improvement conflicts with its complex-band deterioration. Both lines received a new packing-list template; catch recording also changed. Baseline complex share was 1,500/3,600 = 41.667%, versus only 300/3,600 = 8.333% in the mature pilot. Next-period complex share is 30%. Pooling these workloads supports the wrong quality forecast.

| Mature outcome | Alder baseline | Alder pilot | Birch baseline | Birch pilot |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Standard errors / orders | 42/2,100 = 2% | 33/3,300 = 1% | 42/2,100 = 2% | 33/3,300 = 1% |
| Complex errors / orders | 90/1,500 = 6% | 30/300 = 10% | 60/1,500 = 4% | 18/300 = 6% |
| Aggregate | 132/3,600 = 3.6667% | 63/3,600 = 1.75% | 102/3,600 = 2.8333% | 51/3,600 = 1.4167% |
| At planned 70/30 mix | 3.20% | 3.70% | 2.60% | 2.50% |

Weighting each band's error rate by 70/30 gives Alder a 0.5-point deterioration. Birch improves 0.1 point at that same mix; subtracting its change leaves a 0.6-point adverse relative signal. Standards improve equally on both lines, while complex orders worsen more on Alder. This does not prove checking caused harm, nor establish a beneficial standard-only variant. It does defeat a claim that aggregate improvement establishes an incremental checker benefit. The small 300-order complex pilot and baseline line differences further limit causal confidence.

| Full-shift operation | Alder baseline | Alder pilot | Birch baseline | Birch pilot |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Paid productive hours, three shifts | 246 | 275 | 240 | 246 |
| Training subset | 0 | 5 | 0 | 0 |
| Recurring hours per shift | 82 | 90 | 80 | 82 |
| Late / shipped | 21/3,600 = 0.5833% | 42/3,600 = 1.1667% | 18/3,600 = 0.50% | 18/3,600 = 0.50% |

The immature 600-order August 28 shift yields no seven-day quality evidence. Including it incorrectly gives Alder 63/4,200 = 1.50% instead of 1.75%. Its zero late dispatches are real: all-pilot lateness is exactly 42/4,200 = 1%. That pooled observation must not conceal the full-shift 1.1667% result when assessing larger future shifts. Catch-count increases cannot establish improvement because mandatory capture began during launch.

Counting hours once and excluding only one-off training gives 90 recurring Alder hours for 1,200 orders. Constant hours per order would require 90 × 1,440/1,200 = 108 hours, exceeding the hard 102-hour cap. Comparing 90 directly with 102 ignores planned volume. Scaling the short shift's 36 hours to obtain 86.4 hours is unsupported. Band-specific times are unavailable, so neither a mix adjustment nor selective-check capacity can be treated as measured.

At 28,800 planned Alder orders, the comparison-adjusted quality scenario implies 172.8 additional errors, costing $9,504. Adjusted labor increment is [(90−82)−(82−80)] × 1.2 × 20 = 144 hours, costing $4,896; net benefit is −$14,400. A disclosed raw standardized comparison gives −$14,448. These are scenarios, not causal losses. The adjusted no-check hours scenario is 100.8 per shift, close to the cap; it also is not validated at the new mix.

Stopping broad Alder checking, retaining Birch's existing process, and managing the planned workload within the cap is defensible, with early dispatch/staffing review and later mature quality review. A bounded narrow investigation may also work if it states a feasible scope, staffing, interim fallback, timing, and what uncertainty it resolves. A promise that selective checks automatically cost in proportion to order share fails. Unconditional broad continuation or expansion cannot rely on the observed 90-hour shifts, extra unauthorized labor, shifted orders, or an invented dollar price for lateness. An alternative must resolve the cap and dispatch problem before implementation, not merely request evidence while leaving an infeasible default running.

## Acceptance and limits

A successful answer provides an actionable local recommendation, correct populations and units, reviewable calculations, justified planning assumptions, and operational consequences under the actual authority. It need not reproduce every benchmark or use a particular tool. A dashboard without a decision, confident causal attribution, fictitious realized improvement, double-counted labor, or an unsupported feasible-workload claim materially fails the purpose.

`control-case-checks/S09/check_packet_arithmetic.py` independently reads the fixtures with Python's standard library; `arithmetic-output.txt` records its public output. It verifies disjoint cohorts, matching operation keys, eligible outcome counts, and the arithmetic above. These checks establish synthetic internal coherence only. Neither generated Skill effectiveness nor real-world intervention effectiveness has been tested. Both packets contain few shifts, planning rather than guaranteed demand, and no randomized counterfactual or measured selective workflow.
