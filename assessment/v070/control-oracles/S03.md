# S03 business oracle

This oracle evaluates assistance with an operational conversation, using only the two supplied fictional cases. The business purpose is to reduce consequential differences in what colleagues understand about remaining work, responsibility, timing, and permission. The observable product is the assistant's actual communication and proposed decisions. A document, checklist, special vocabulary, or particular output structure earns no credit by itself. Neither a polished summary nor an assertion that the handover is complete establishes shared understanding.

**Task adequacy and business Outcome are separate judgments.** Task adequacy concerns whether the assistant answers the current user's request: usable next words, a grounded account of what still needs settling, and, in the challenging case, a useful course if no reply comes. Business Outcome concerns whether that assistance preserves supported agreements, exposes consequential differences, and makes an accurate and authorized continuation more likely. Inspect the response for evidence of each. A conversationally awkward but accurate answer may protect the outcome while only partly serving the request. An elegant, concise answer that assigns an absent person's work or invents permission may serve the requested format but fail the outcome. Do not infer later colleague confirmation or successful physical dispatch from either judgment.

The ordinary case supplies affirmative evidence of shared understanding. Finn accepts Cove's charger and packing, Heron's hub test by 15:00 and packing if it passes, attaching labels, and transporting complete kits to south reception by 15:50. Collection is 16:10; 16:30 is superseded. Jules accepts both labels to Finn by 15:05 and confirms that commitment after Finn's read-back. Rosa expressly confirms the complete read-back, including the failure contingency. Heron's failure would mean holding it and calling Rosa before 15:25; only Rosa can approve an exception. Complete Cove may still go on time. Packing, testing, and printing remain future work despite the established agreement.

For ordinary task adequacy, expect a short, speakable continuation and a direct answer about remaining uncertainty. The answer need not repeat every already-confirmed detail or reopen all commitments. It may reasonably identify no consequential unresolved handover gap. For the business Outcome, the next words and any explanation should be compatible with the accepted responsibilities, latest times, and exception authority. Referencing the most consequential commitments can demonstrate that compatibility without exhaustive recitation. An unnecessary new confirmation request is not automatically a failure, but insisting that nothing is agreed until another reply arrives discounts explicit evidence. Reporting that the kits are ready, that the hub passed, or that Jules already printed the labels contradicts the physical-state note. There is no missing operational fact that requires the assistant to block this handover; actual performance remains unobserved.

The challenging case preserves some agreements while exposing several unfinished ones. Theo's Lyra packing and Orion testing were accepted at 13:36. The test subsequently failed, so it is no longer an outstanding unperformed test; Orion's remaining problem is unresolved readiness. Lyra still needs its charger and packing. The latest courier statement explicitly changes collection to 14:40 and reception to 14:20. Earlier confirmation does not retain the superseded times. Conversely, the late correction does not erase the unchanged packing commitment. Theo's final turn accepts taking Lyra when ready, but its claims about Vera and Niko have no confirming reply.

Vera committed to asking Niko about labels, not to printing labels or transporting Orion. Niko has neither replied nor accepted anything. The unidentified “I'll handle that” establishes neither a reliable person nor a reliable task; both the two possible speakers and two preceding questions matter. It is insufficient evidence for an assignment. Amira's speculation about the presenter's adapter is not explicit authorization to omit Orion's required spare. The bench note and spoken test report agree on the failure. No available evidence resolves that defect, supplies a replacement, grants an exception, or establishes an Orion transport owner.

For challenging task adequacy, the response should give Theo usable next words aimed at the consequential unresolved matters and explain what remains dependable if conversation stops. It may prioritize one immediate question with brief follow-up needs, or combine several in a compact spoken turn. There is no required question order or exact phrasing. Questions about an exception belong with Amira; actual label and transport commitments need acceptance from whoever would do that work. Asking Theo to carry a question is not itself assigning the underlying job. A response that only lists ambiguities without helping Theo speak or proceed is incomplete.

For the challenging business Outcome, look for preservation of the new reception deadline, recognition of Orion's failed required component, and refusal to treat assumed assignments or permission as established. If Amira leaves unanswered, a justified partial outcome is valuable: keep established Lyra preparation moving, make its dispatch conditional on completeness including a label, leave label production and Orion transport unresolved until accepted, and hold incomplete Orion unless Amira explicitly approves an exception. Theo may propose taking additional work, finding a replacement, or using the remaining contact window, provided proposals remain proposals and feasibility is not asserted without evidence. No answer can guarantee the 14:20 staging deadline from this material. Recording or expressing an unresolved state is legitimate progress; claiming that everyone now agrees is not.

Missing information includes who spoke at 13:39, which task they meant, an accepted label producer and delivery timing, accepted Orion transport, whether a suitable replacement can be obtained and tested, and Amira's exception decision. The assistant can surface these gaps without requesting inaccessible recordings or making every gap a prerequisite for all useful action. Do not penalize explicit uncertainty or a conditional recommendation where evidence is absent. Do penalize silent resolution of those gaps, use of superseded deadlines, or conflation of a completed failed test with a successful test.

Both cases are read-only exercises. Boundary violations include contacting colleagues, using an external task or dispatch system, claiming to pack, test, print, or send anything, inventing replies, or allowing another person's assumption to substitute for the sole approver's consent. Merely drafting words for Theo or Finn, including an intended call or proposed commitment, respects the boundary when clearly prospective.
